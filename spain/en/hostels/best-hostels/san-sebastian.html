Best Hostels in San Sebastián

San Sebastián is one of Spain's most beautiful coastal cities, with surf beaches, spectacular scenery, a legendary pintxos scene and a nightlife that tends to revolve around eating and drinking rather than getting absolutely wrecked in a hostel bar.

It's also one of the more expensive places to stay in northern Spain, so choosing the right hostel can make a serious difference to your budget. Whether you're here to surf Zurriola Beach, explore the Old Town, meet other backpackers or simply find somewhere decent to sleep without selling a kidney, these are our picks of the best hostels in San Sebastián.

We've looked beyond the headline ratings, taking into account location, atmosphere, cleanliness, facilities, value for money and what previous guests actually say about their stays.

Please note: The hostels do not pay us to be on this list!

  • Best central hostel: A Room in the City

The best hostels in San Sebastián

Koba Hostel
Best overall hostel

  • Booking.com: 8.7/10 (2,009 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com: 9.2/10 (1,089 reviews)
  • Google: 4.6/5 (562 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Koba is one of the strongest all-round performers in San Sebastián. Guests consistently praise the exceptionally friendly staff, cleanliness, comfortable beds and included breakfast. Its atmosphere is sociable without being particularly rowdy, so don't expect a full-on party hostel.

Best for: Backpackers who want a comfortable, friendly hostel with excellent facilities, a good breakfast and easy access to the beach.

Housed in a converted former car workshop in Gros, Koba Hostel is a smart, modern place with a surprisingly relaxed feel. It's about two minutes from Zurriola Beach, one of San Sebastián's main surf spots, and around ten minutes on foot from the Old Town.

The dorm beds have privacy curtains, individual lights and sockets, personal lockers and en-suite bathrooms. There's also a communal kitchen, terrace, lounge, cinema corner and workspaces. Breakfast is included, which is a particularly welcome bonus in a city where eating out can get expensive.

The hostel also organises activities including surfing, kayaking, cycling and hiking. It's a good place to meet people, although the general atmosphere is more relaxed than party-focused.

Not for you if: You're looking for a hostel with nightly parties, a big drinking scene or a bar that stays lively until the early hours.

  • Area: Gros
  • Nearest station: Donostia train and bus station, approximately 8 minutes on foot
  • Address: Calle Carquizano, 5, Bajo, 20001 Donostia-San Sebastián, Spain
  • Website: kobahostel.com
  • Phone: +34 943 165 817
  • Email: info@kobahostel.com

A Room in the City
Best central hostel

  • Booking.com: 8.4/10 (4,091 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com: 8.7/10 (3,003 reviews)
  • Google: 4.3/5 (1,989 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: The biggest strength here is location. Guests consistently rate it highly for its central position, cleanliness, comfortable beds and social spaces. Its atmosphere score is a little more moderate, suggesting a sociable hostel rather than a dedicated party destination.

Best for: Backpackers who want to be right in the middle of the action, close to the beach, Old Town, restaurants and nightlife.

A Room in the City occupies part of the Convent Garden complex, a former convent turned into a lively social space in the heart of San Sebastián. It's just a short walk from La Concha Beach and within easy walking distance of the Old Town and its famous pintxos bars.

The hostel has a mix of shared and private rooms, indoor and outdoor communal areas, a shared kitchen and access to the complex's bar and terrace. It's a sizeable operation, with plenty of opportunities to meet other travellers and a location that's hard to beat.

One thing worth knowing is that breakfast isn't included, and some guests have mentioned that rooms can become hot or that nearby nightlife can create noise. It's also not a traditional party hostel: stag and hen groups aren't accepted.

Not for you if: You want a small, intimate hostel, a guaranteed party atmosphere or somewhere completely removed from the noise of the city centre.

  • Area: City centre
  • Nearest station: Amara station, approximately 5 minutes on foot
  • Address: Easo Kalea, 20 / Manterola Kalea, 15, 20006 Donostia-San Sebastián, Spain
  • Website: aroominthecity.eu
  • Phone: +34 943 42 95 89
  • Email: info@aroominthecity.eu

Surfing Etxea Surf Hostel
Best for surfers

  • Booking.com: 8.5/10 (454 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com: 8.8/10 (916 reviews)
  • Google: 4.5/5 (55 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Surfing Etxea scores particularly well for location and staff. Guests like the friendly atmosphere, helpful team and proximity to the beach. It's a good choice for travellers who want a surf-oriented hostel, although some reviews mention that the common areas and social atmosphere can be fairly low-key.

Best for: Surfers, beach lovers and solo travellers who want to spend time around Zurriola Beach and meet people with similar interests.

Located in Gros, just a couple of minutes from Zurriola Beach, Surfing Etxea is a small surf-focused hostel with a relaxed, friendly atmosphere. The beach is one of the city's main surfing spots, and the hostel offers surf lessons and equipment rental, making it a particularly convenient base if you're planning to spend time in the water.

There's a bar and café, social space, bicycle facilities and activities connected with surfing. The hostel is also within walking distance of the Old Town, although it's far enough away to feel more like a beach-and-surf base than a city-centre party hostel.

The smaller size can be a plus if you prefer a more personal atmosphere, but don't expect the extensive facilities of a large hostel. Some guests have also mentioned the lack of air conditioning in rooms during hot weather.

Not for you if: You want a large hostel with a big kitchen, extensive communal facilities or a lively bar scene every night.

  • Area: Gros
  • Nearest station: Donostia train and bus station, approximately 15 minutes on foot
  • Address: Zabaleta Kalea, 32, 20002 Donostia-San Sebastián, Spain
  • Website: sansebastiansurfhostel.com
  • Phone: +34 943 265 832
  • Email: info@surfingetxea.com

Colo Colo the Smart Hostel
Best for solo travellers

  • Booking.com: 8.8/10 (1,637 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com: 8.7/10 (160 reviews)
  • Google: 4.6/5 (187 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Colo Colo has very strong feedback for its location, privacy and modern facilities. Guests particularly like the individual sleeping pods, comfortable beds and quiet sleeping environment. Its relatively small number of Hostelworld reviews means that rating is based on a smaller sample than some of the city's more established hostels.

Best for: Solo travellers who value privacy, a good night's sleep and a central location, but still want the option of meeting other backpackers.

Colo Colo is a slightly different take on the traditional hostel. Instead of conventional dorm beds, it offers individual sleeping pods called Casitas. Each has its own tablet, headphones, charging points and secure locker, giving you considerably more privacy than a normal shared dorm.

The hostel is right in the centre of San Sebastián, within easy walking distance of La Concha Beach, Zurriola Beach and the Old Town. There's also a communal area called the Agora, where guests can hang out and meet other travellers, with free coffee available.

It's a modern, technology-focused place designed around comfort and privacy. The sleeping area has quiet hours, with lights switched off at midnight, so it's not the place to go if you're hoping for a late-night party in the hostel itself.

Not for you if: You want a conventional hostel dorm, a full communal kitchen, private rooms or a lively late-night atmosphere. The sleeping pods are individual beds in a shared sleeping area, not private rooms.

CATS Hostel San Sebastián
Best near Ondarreta Beach

  • Booking.com: 8.3/10 (3,200 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com: 8.7/10 (39 reviews)
  • Google: 4.2/5 (1,442 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: CATS gets good feedback for cleanliness, comfortable rooms and helpful staff. The main compromise is its location: it's in the Antiguo neighbourhood, rather than the central Gros or Old Town areas. Hostelworld's rating is based on a relatively small number of reviews compared with Booking.com and Google.

Best for: Travellers who prefer a modern, comfortable hostel near Ondarreta Beach and don't mind being a little further from the Old Town.

Formerly known as Koisi Hostel, CATS is a modern hostel in the Antiguo neighbourhood, a short walk from Ondarreta Beach and the bars and restaurants around Matia Kalea.

The property has air-conditioned rooms, communal areas, a bar and modern facilities. It's a good option if you'd rather stay in a quieter neighbourhood and explore the western side of the city, rather than being right in the middle of the Old Town nightlife.

It's worth knowing that the hostel is not particularly close to the historic centre, and recent guest feedback suggests that the social atmosphere can be fairly quiet. Some travellers also mention the lack of a full communal kitchen and privacy curtains on dorm beds.

Not for you if: You want to be within stumbling distance of the Old Town's bars, prefer a hostel with a lively backpacker atmosphere or rely on cooking your own meals.

Where to stay in San Sebastián

San Sebastián is compact enough to explore on foot, but the neighbourhood you choose can make a difference to your stay. The main areas to consider are Gros, the Old Town and the Antiguo neighbourhood.

Gros

Gros is the best area for backpackers who are interested in surfing, beach life and a relaxed social atmosphere. Zurriola Beach is the city's main surf beach, and the neighbourhood has plenty of cafés, bars and restaurants. Koba Hostel and Surfing Etxea are both here.

Old Town and city centre

The city centre and Parte Vieja (Old Town) are the best places to stay if you want to be close to the pintxos bars, restaurants, shopping streets and nightlife. A Room in the City and Colo Colo are both well placed for exploring the city on foot.

Antiguo

Antiguo is a quieter neighbourhood on the western side of the city, close to Ondarreta Beach. It's a good option if you'd rather be near the beach than the Old Town, although you'll have a longer walk or bus ride to reach the main nightlife areas. CATS Hostel is located here.

How much do hostels cost in San Sebastián?

San Sebastián is one of the more expensive destinations in northern Spain, particularly during the summer months and major local events. Dorm prices can vary considerably depending on the season, how far ahead you book and whether there's a festival or other event taking place.

As a general rule, expect to pay more for a bed in the city centre or close to the beach than you would in a less central neighbourhood. Booking early is particularly important during July and August.

Remember to compare the total cost rather than just the bed price. A hostel that includes breakfast, has a kitchen or is within walking distance of the places you want to visit can offer better value than a cheaper bed further out.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best hostel in San Sebastián?

Koba Hostel is our best overall pick. It combines excellent guest reviews, friendly staff, good facilities, included breakfast and a great location in the Gros neighbourhood.

What is the best area to stay in San Sebastián?

Gros is a great choice for backpackers, particularly surfers, while the city centre and Old Town are best if you want to be close to restaurants, pintxos bars and nightlife.

Is San Sebastián good for solo travellers?

Yes. Its hostels make it relatively easy to meet other travellers, and the city is compact and walkable. Koba Hostel and A Room in the City are particularly good options for a sociable stay. Colo Colo is a better choice if you value privacy and a quieter sleeping environment.

Is San Sebastián a good place to learn to surf?

Yes. Zurriola Beach in Gros is the city's main surf beach, and Surfing Etxea is a convenient base if you're interested in lessons, equipment rental and spending time around the local surf scene.

Is San Sebastián expensive for backpackers?

It can be. Accommodation and eating out are generally more expensive than in many other Spanish destinations. Staying in a hostel with a kitchen, booking ahead and making the most of the city's beaches and free outdoor attractions can help keep costs down.
