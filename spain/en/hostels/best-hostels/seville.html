The Best Hostels in Seville

Ratings, prices and availability checked: 8 November 2026.

It is also one of the best cities in Spain for backpackers who want a social hostel without quite committing to the full-blown, 3am, someone-has-stolen-my-sleeping-bag chaos of Barcelona or Madrid. Seville’s hostel scene is smaller, warmer, and more sociable — less “club until sunrise”, more “tapas, rooftop drinks, flamenco, and someone insisting that you simply must try the jamón”.

The best hostel for you depends on one question: do you want a rooftop pool, a ready-made group of friends, a cheap bed in Triana, or a hostel that feels like it was designed by someone who has actually travelled?

Most important item: A fan. Or a personality that can survive 40°C.

How we chose these hostels

This guide covers the best party hostels, social hostels, budget picks and comfort stays in Seville, with neighbourhood advice, typical prices, and booking tips.

We include direct contact details because not everyone wants to book through an affiliate platform. Prices, availability and ratings change, so always confirm directly with the hostel before booking.

The Best Hostels in Seville

Seville’s hostels are built around a simple idea: you arrive hot, sweaty and slightly overwhelmed by the beauty of the place, and someone immediately offers you a drink, a walking tour, or a reason to stay out until midnight.

La Banda Rooftop Hostel

  • Booking.com Rating: 9.8 / 10 (1,248 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 9.6 / 10 (5,401 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.7 / 5 Stars (806 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: La Banda Rooftop Hostel’s exceptionally strong ratings reflect its unbeatable combination of social atmosphere, friendly staff, cleanliness and location. Guests consistently highlight the rooftop dinners, organised activities, free walking tours and easygoing international crowd, all of which make it one of Seville’s best hostels for meeting people. The Hostelworld scores are particularly impressive: 9.8 for security, 9.8 for staff, 9.9 for location and 9.5 for both atmosphere and cleanliness, suggesting that La Banda manages to be social, well run and comfortable at the same time. Its rooftop bar, family dinners, live music, flamenco nights and pub crawls make it an obvious choice for backpackers who want to arrive alone and leave with a crew.

Best overall party hostel. La Banda is Seville’s most obvious choice for backpackers who want a social base with a proper nightlife pulse. It has a rooftop terrace, regular events, a young international crowd, and a location that makes it easy to turn “one drink” into “why is it light outside?”

Best for: Backpackers who want to meet people, drink on a roof, and treat the Alameda as a natural extension of the hostel.

Not for you if: Not for you if: You want silence, early nights, or a hostel where the loudest thing is a kettle.

Onefam Catedral
Best hostel for solo travellers

  • Booking.com Rating: 7.9 / 10 (893 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 9.5 / 10 (5,498 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.3 / 5 Stars (638 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Onefam Catedral’s strong Hostelworld rating is driven mainly by its location, staff and social atmosphere. Guests consistently mention the daily activities, communal dinners, pub crawls and informal get-togethers that make it easy to meet people, while the hostel’s position beside the cathedral puts the Giralda, Alcázar, Santa Cruz and central tapas bars within a short walk. The 9.9 location score and 9.8 staff score are particularly strong, and reviews often highlight the rooftop, kitchen and historic building. It is a good choice for solo travellers who want a ready-made group, though a few guests note that some facilities feel more basic or worn than the social experience suggests.

Best for: First-time solo travellers, people who want a built-in social group, and anyone who likes the idea of communal dinners without having to cook.

Onefam Catedral is the hostel equivalent of arriving in a new city and immediately being adopted by a group of people who already know where the cheap drinks are. It is social, welcoming, well organised, and close enough to the cathedral that you can pretend your hangover is cultural.

Not for you if: You want a quiet, anonymous hotel-style stay.

JOY Setas Coworking Hostel Sevilla
best social hostel with a rooftop pool and coworking space

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.7 / 10 (940 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 9.1 / 10 (2,708 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.2 / 5 Stars (478 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: JOY Setas Coworking’s strong ratings reflect its central location, clean facilities, rooftop pool and unusually broad mix of hostel, coworking and social spaces. Guests consistently praise the staff, the views over Las Setas, the rooftop terrace and pool, and the balance between meeting people and having somewhere quiet to work. The 9.6 location score and 9.7 security score are particularly strong, while the 9.3 staff score reflects a team that guests describe as helpful rather than constantly organising a party. It is a good choice for solo travellers, digital nomads and anyone who wants a social base without committing to a hostel built entirely around drinking.

Best for: Solo travellers, digital nomads and backpackers who want a social base without committing to a hostel built entirely around drinking. JOY Setas Coworking is best for travellers who want to meet people, use a proper coworking space, enjoy a rooftop pool and still be close to Seville’s main sights. It is social enough to make friends easily, but not so relentless that you feel guilty for wanting an early night. The location beside Las Setas puts the cathedral, Alcázar, Santa Cruz and central tapas bars within walking distance, while the rooftop terrace and pool give you somewhere to recover from the Andalusian heat.

Not for you if: You want the cheapest possible bed, a hostel built entirely around drinking, or a party atmosphere that starts before breakfast. JOY Setas is social, but it is not a hardcore party hostel. It is also not the best choice if you want a quiet, hotel-style stay with no shared spaces, or if you need a large, private room with lots of storage. The hostel is designed around communal areas, a shared kitchen and a social atmosphere, so privacy is limited compared with a guesthouse or hotel. If you want to be in the middle of the old town, it is central but not right beside the cathedral; expect a short walk to the main sights.

    Best hostel for wheelchair users

    • Google Places Rating: 3.9 / 5 Stars (1,003 reviews)

    What the ratings tell you: Albergue Inturjoven Sevilla stands out because its accessibility provision is specific rather than vague. The hostel has 16 rooms adapted for sensory disabilities, including eight adapted for reduced mobility or wheelchair users, as well as accessible parking and detailed accessibility information. It is less central than Seville’s main backpacker hostels, but it is the clearest choice for travellers who need a room designed for wheelchair use rather than simply a lift and a step-free entrance.

    Where to Stay in Seville

    Santa Cruz and the Cathedral

    Stay here if you want to be in the middle of Seville’s postcard version of itself: narrow streets, orange trees, tapas bars, flamenco, and enough tourists to make you question whether you are also a tourist. Onefam Catedral is the obvious hostel choice nearby.

    Alameda de Hércules

    Stay here if you want bars, terraces, alternative crowds, and a more local-feeling nightlife scene. La Banda Rooftop Hostel is the natural base for this area.

    Triana

    Stay here if you want a more local, less touristy Seville, with flamenco, river walks, and a proper neighbourhood feel. Triana Backpackers is the obvious choice.

    Las Setas and the Centre

    Stay here if you want to be central, walk everywhere, and have easy access to bars, sights, and public transport. Oasis Backpackers Sevilla is well placed for this.

    Plaza de Armas

    Stay here if you want a larger, more social hostel with a pool, and do not mind being slightly away from the most atmospheric old-town streets. Oasis Backpackers’ Palace is the main option.

    How Much Do Hostels in Seville Cost?

    Most dorm beds in Seville cost roughly €15–€30 per night, with budget options in Triana and the outskirts sometimes cheaper. Design-led hostels, private rooms and peak-season dates can push prices higher.

    Seville gets seriously hot in summer. Air conditioning is not a luxury; it is a public health measure. Book early for spring, autumn, Semana Santa, Feria de Abril, and any weekend when the weather is good enough for everyone in Europe to decide they need sunshine immediately.

    For a three-night stay, a realistic backpacker budget is around €180–€300, including a dorm bed, food, drinks, transport and a couple of nights out.

    Seville Hostel FAQs

    What is the best party hostel in Seville?

    La Banda Rooftop Hostel is the best party hostel in Seville for most backpackers. It has a rooftop terrace, regular events, a social crowd, and a location that makes it easy to turn one drink into an entire evening.

    What is the best hostel in Seville for solo travellers?

    Onefam Catedral is the best hostel for solo travellers. It is social, welcoming, well located, and designed to make it easy to meet people without having to stand in a kitchen holding a beer and hoping someone speaks English.

    What is the best area to stay in Seville for backpackers?

    Stay between the cathedral, Santa Cruz, Alameda and the centre if you want the best all-round base. Choose Triana if you want a more local feel and cheaper beds.

    How much is a hostel in Seville?

    Expect roughly €15–€30 per night for a dorm bed, with higher prices in spring, autumn, weekends and during major festivals.

    Is Seville safe for solo travellers?

    Yes. Seville is generally safe, friendly, and easy to navigate. The usual rules apply: watch your belongings in crowded areas, do not leave your phone on a café table, and do not assume that a charming stranger offering to show you “the real Seville” is necessarily doing so out of civic pride.

    When should I book a hostel in Seville?

    Book early for Semana Santa and Feria de Abril. These are not merely busy periods; they are events during which the entire city appears to have been rented out by someone with very specific ideas about tradition, dresses, horses, and drinking before noon.
