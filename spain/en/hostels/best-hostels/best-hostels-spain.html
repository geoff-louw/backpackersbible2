The Best Hostels in Spain

The Backpacker’s Bible Hostel Awards 2026. Ratings taken from our city guides, checked between 7 and 27 November 2026.

We have written a “best hostels” guide for every major backpacker city in Spain and every popular Camino route: twelve guides and more than 80 hand-picked hostels. This page puts them all head to head. Each city sends its champion to a regional round, each region sends its champion to a national final, and the best of the best is crowned Backpacker’s Bible Hostel of the Year.

Not everyone wants the same thing from a hostel, so we also hand out twelve category awards: best party hostel, best for solo travellers, best rooftop, best pool, best value, best on the Camino, best for female travellers and more.

The winners at a glance

  • Hostel of the Year: Onefam Paralelo, Barcelona

  • Runner-up: Onefam Sungate, Madrid

  • Third place: La Banda Rooftop Hostel, Seville


  • Best party hostel in Spain: Kabul Party Hostel, Barcelona

  • Best hostel for solo travellers: Onefam Sants, Barcelona

  • Best hostel for groups: OK Hostel Madrid, Madrid

  • Best rooftop terrace: The Hat Madrid, Madrid

  • Best rooftop pool: The Central House Lavapiés, Madrid

  • Best value hostel: El Granado Hostel, Granada

  • Best hostel on the Camino de Santiago: Hostel Charino, Camino Portugués

  • Most characterful hostel: Oripandó Hostel, Granada

  • Best design hostel: The Loft House Sevilla, Seville

  • Best hostel for digital nomads: JOY Setas Coworking Hostel Sevilla, Seville

  • Best surf hostel: Surfing Etxea Surf Hostel, San Sebastián

  • Best hostel for female travellers: Lamia BBK Women Hostel, Bilbao

Please note: The hostels do not pay us to be on this list, and they cannot pay to win!

How the Awards Work

Every hostel on this page was first chosen as a pick in one of our city or Camino guides. Those 80-odd hostels were then scored and put through three rounds.

The Backpacker’s Bible Score

Every hostel gets a single score out of 10. It combines its Booking.com, Hostelworld and Google ratings (Google’s five stars are doubled to make them out of 10) and weights each one by its number of reviews. A 9.9 from 8,000 reviews counts for far more than a 10 from 50.

To stop small hostels with a handful of glowing reviews from running away with the prizes, every hostel also starts with 1,000 “neutral” reviews at 8.5. A hostel needs a large number of genuinely great reviews to climb well above that. That rewards consistency: a hostel that delights thousands of backpackers, year after year.

Three rounds

  • Round 1, city champions: The highest-scoring hostel in each of our 12 guides wins its city or Camino route.
  • Round 2, regional champions: City champions in the same region go head to head, giving six regional champions: Catalonia, Madrid, Andalusia, Valencia, the Basque Country, and Galicia & the Camino.
  • Round 3, the national final: The six regional champions compete for Hostel of the Year. The top three take gold, silver and bronze. Ties at two decimal places go to the hostel with more reviews.

Category awards

Category awards are open to every hostel in our guides except the three medal winners, and no hostel can win more than one award. For each category, we looked at the hostels with the relevant feature (a rooftop, a pool, a surf school, a coworking space and so on) or the relevant Vibe Meter rating (for example, 9 or more for party), and gave the award to the one with the highest Backpacker’s Bible Score. “Most characterful hostel” is the one editorial award, and we explain our choice.

Ratings, prices and availability change all the time, so always check the latest reviews and confirm directly with the hostel before booking.

Onefam Paralelo
Hostel of the Year 2026 (Gold)

  • Backpacker’s Bible Score: 9.69 / 10 (from 10,493 reviews)
  • Booking.com Rating: 9.5 / 10 (1,200 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 9.9 / 10 (8,599 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.6 / 5 Stars (694 reviews)

Why it won: Onefam Paralelo has the highest Backpacker’s Bible Score of any hostel in Spain. Its 9.9 Hostelworld rating comes from almost 8,600 reviews, and it backs that up with 9.5 on Booking.com and 4.6 stars on Google. Scoring that high from that many reviews on three different platforms is very hard to do.

Best for: Solo backpackers on a budget who want free dinners, a ready-made crew and Barcelona nightlife without Gothic Quarter prices.

Onefam Paralelo is in Poble Sec, close to Sala Apolo and the cheap pintxos bars of Carrer de Blai. It has a party room, lounge, coworking space and nightly activities, but the big draw is the free family dinner: eat together first, then head out on the hostel night out. It is the kind of place where you arrive alone and leave with a WhatsApp group.

Not for you if: you want peace and quiet or a polished, hotel-style stay. This is a social hostel first and foremost.

  • Area: Poble Sec, Barcelona
  • Nearest metro / station: Paral·lel (L2, L3)
  • Address: Carrer de Salvà, 62, Sants-Montjuïc, 08004 Barcelona

Onefam Sungate
Runner-up (Silver)

  • Backpacker’s Bible Score: 9.63 / 10 (from 11,637 reviews)
  • Booking.com Rating: 9.4 / 10 (2,547 reviews)

Why it won: Onefam Sungate finished a whisker behind its Barcelona sister. It has the best Booking.com (9.4) and Google (4.7) scores of the three finalists, and a 9.9 on Hostelworld from more than 7,600 reviews.

Best for: First-time solo backpackers who want a smaller hostel with a built-in social group, three minutes from Puerta del Sol.

Sungate removes the usual hostel barriers: there are no bunk beds, rooms feel more like a simple hotel, and the staff actively bring guests together. Free group dinners, daily activities, pub crawls and a shared kitchen make it easy to meet people, and you are in the middle of Madrid’s old centre, tapas bars and nightlife.

Not for you if: you want a huge party hostel with hundreds of guests. Cats Party Hostel will give you a bigger crowd.

Kabul Party Hostel
Best party hostel in Spain

  • Backpacker’s Bible Score: 9.17 / 10 (from 18,296 reviews)
  • Booking.com Rating: 8.5 / 10 (3,916 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 9.5 / 10 (11,647 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.5 / 5 Stars (2,733 reviews)

Why it won: Kabul beat OK Hostel Madrid by the narrowest of margins (9.17 vs 9.16) among hostels with a party rating of 9 or more on our Vibe Meter. With more than 18,000 reviews, it is also one of the most reviewed hostels in Spain.

Best for: Backpackers who want to be in the middle of Barcelona’s nightlife from the moment they arrive.

Kabul sits directly on Plaça Reial, just off La Rambla. It has a rooftop terrace, bar, pool table, free breakfast, daily walking tours and a nightly pub crawl. If location, atmosphere and meeting people who are there to party are your priorities, this is the hostel to book.

Not for you if: you are a light sleeper or want a quiet, polished hostel. Recent reviews mention noise and variable cleanliness.

Not for you if: you want to stumble home from a club in two minutes. The Gothic Quarter is roughly 20 minutes away.

Not for you if: you want a sleek design hostel or a quiet, boutique-style stay.

Lavapiés is one of Madrid’s most interesting neighbourhoods: multicultural and full of street art and bars, within walking distance of El Rastro, Sol and the Reina Sofía.

In a city where tapas come free with your drink, it is a budget backpacker’s dream.

Hostel Charino is a small hostel of around two dozen beds right in the historic centre, which makes it an excellent base for an evening exploring on foot after a day’s walk.

Not for you if: you are looking for a big, sociable party hostel with pub crawls and nightly events.

Staying among the Albaicín’s whitewashed lanes and hidden plazas is half the point.

Why it won: JOY Setas is the best-rated hostel in our guides with a proper coworking space. It scores 9.1 on Hostelworld from more than 2,700 reviews, with 9.6 for location and 9.7 for security, and it edges out Hostel Nut in Granada.

Best for: Digital nomads and solo travellers who want to work in the day and socialise at night.

JOY Setas combines a hostel, a coworking space and a rooftop terrace with a pool overlooking Las Setas. It is social enough to make friends easily, but not so relentless that you feel guilty for wanting an early night. The cathedral, Alcázar, Santa Cruz and central tapas bars are all within walking distance.

Not for you if: you want the cheapest bed in town or a hardcore party hostel.

Best Hostels in Spain FAQs

What is the best hostel in Spain?

Onefam Paralelo in Barcelona is the Backpacker’s Bible Hostel of the Year 2026. It has a 9.9 Hostelworld rating from almost 8,600 reviews, 9.5 on Booking.com and 4.6 on Google. Onefam Sungate in Madrid came second and La Banda Rooftop Hostel in Seville came third.

Which Spanish city has the best hostels?

Barcelona. Its top three hostels (Onefam Paralelo, Onefam Sants and Kabul) have the highest average score of any city in our guides, followed by Madrid, Granada and Seville.

What is the best party hostel in Spain?

Kabul Party Hostel on Plaça Reial in Barcelona, by a whisker from OK Hostel Madrid. If you are heading south, La Banda Rooftop Hostel in Seville is the joint highest-rated hostel in Spain outside the Onefam group.

What is the best hostel in Spain for solo travellers?

Any Onefam hostel. Their free family dinners, daily activities and nights out make it almost impossible to stay on your own. Onefam Sants won the solo traveller award, and Onefam Paralelo and Onefam Sungate took gold and silver overall.

What is the best hostel in Spain for female travellers?

Lamia BBK Women Hostel in Bilbao is women-only, with outstanding security, cleanliness and staff ratings. Many other hostels in our guides offer female-only dorms, so check the room type when booking.

What is the best hostel on the Camino de Santiago?

Hostel Charino in Pontevedra, on the Camino Portugués, has a 9.7 Booking.com rating and a perfect 5.0 on Google. On the Camino Francés, Albergue PortoSantiago in Portomarín is the highest-rated stop in our guide.

How do you choose the winners?

Every hostel first has to make one of our city or Camino guides. We then calculate a single Backpacker’s Bible Score from its Booking.com, Hostelworld and Google ratings, weighted by number of reviews, and run three rounds: city champions, regional champions and a national final. See How the Awards Work for the full method.

Do hostels pay to be included?

No. Hostels cannot pay to be included or to win an award. If you book through our Booking.com or Hostelworld links, we may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you, which helps keep this guide independent.

How often are the awards updated?

Every year, using the latest ratings from our city guides. Ratings change constantly, so always check recent reviews before you book.
