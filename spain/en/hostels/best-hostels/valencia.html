The Best Hostels in Valencia

Ratings, prices and availability checked: 15 November 2026.

Valencia is one of Spain's best backpacker cities, with a combination that's difficult to beat: a walkable old town, a genuinely good hostel scene, cheap tapas, late-night bars, proper city beaches and some of the strangest architecture you'll find anywhere in Europe. It's also noticeably easier on the wallet than Barcelona or Madrid.

If you want to meet people and join the nightlife, Purple Nest Hostel is a strong choice, with organised pub crawls and regular social events. For an exceptionally sociable stay in the historic centre, Hôme Youth Hostel is hard to beat. If you'd rather have a relaxed atmosphere and a female-only dorm option, Cantagua Hostel is worth a look.

This guide covers Valencia's best party hostels, social hostels, solo-traveller favourites and more comfortable boutique options. We've included neighbourhood advice, typical prices, guest ratings and direct contact details, so you can compare the options and book whichever suits you best.

For the complete accommodation directory, see our full Valencia backpacking guide.

How we chose these hostels

We compared Valencia hostels using guest ratings across Booking.com, Hostelworld and Google, as well as location, social atmosphere, facilities, transport links, value for money and direct booking options. We prioritised places that make sense for backpackers: sociable common areas, secure accommodation, useful locations and opportunities to meet other travellers.

The Vibe Meter scores are our own editorial assessments of each hostel's location, party potential, cleanliness, activities, value and friendliness. They are intended to help you work out what kind of experience to expect, rather than simply repeat the booking-platform ratings.

We include direct contact details because not everyone wants to book through an affiliate platform. Prices, availability and ratings change, so always confirm the details before booking.

Best Hostels in Valencia

Valencia's hostel scene is a good mix of old-school backpacker accommodation and newer boutique-style places. Most of the best-known hostels are within walking distance of the historic centre, although a couple of the more interesting options are a little further out. Here are our picks.

Hôme Youth Hostel
Best overall hostel in Valencia

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.6 / 10 (1,556 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 9.4 / 10 (5,828 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.5 / 5 Stars (749 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Hôme Youth Hostel's excellent Hostelworld score reflects its friendly staff, central location and unusually sociable atmosphere. Guests consistently praise the activities and the opportunity to meet other travellers. Its dormitories also have proper single beds rather than conventional bunk beds, which is a welcome bonus. Booking.com reviews are a little more reserved, but still rate the hostel very highly.

Best for: Solo backpackers and first-time visitors who want a friendly, sociable hostel in the middle of Valencia's historic centre.

Hôme Youth Hostel sits directly opposite La Lonja de la Seda, the city's spectacular old Silk Exchange, and just a few steps from the Central Market. It has a colourful, characterful interior, small dormitories with single beds instead of bunks, private rooms, a shared kitchen and daily activities. The staff organise events to help guests meet one another, making it an especially good choice if you're travelling alone.

Not for you if: You want a large party hostel with a bar and a nightclub atmosphere. Hôme is social and lively, but its appeal is more about meeting people and exploring the city than partying until sunrise.

  • Area: Ciutat Vella / Barrio del Carmen
  • Nearest metro: Xàtiva (L3, L5, L9)
  • Address: Carrer de la Llotja, 4, Ciutat Vella, 46001 València
  • Website: Official Hôme Youth Hostel website / direct bookings
  • Phone: +34 963 91 62 29
  • Email: youthhostel@homehostelsvalencia.com

Purple Nest Hostel
Best party hostel in Valencia

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.1 / 10 (2,322 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 8.7 / 10 (5,475 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.1 / 5 Stars (1,926 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Purple Nest's strong Hostelworld rating reflects its central location, friendly staff and organised social programme. Its atmosphere score is good rather than exceptional, so this isn't necessarily a full-on party hostel every night. However, the regular pub crawls, themed evenings and group activities make it one of Valencia's better choices if you want to meet people and experience the nightlife.

Best for: Backpackers who want organised nights out, pub crawls and a sociable atmosphere without having to organise everything themselves.

Purple Nest is in the heart of Valencia, close to the Turia Gardens and within easy walking distance of the old town's main attractions. The hostel organises free walking tours, Spanish nights, games and drinks evenings, as well as regular pub crawls to some of the city's bars and clubs. There's also a guest kitchen, coworking space and communal areas where you can meet other travellers. It's a good balance between a party hostel and a comfortable city-centre base.

Not for you if: You want a completely quiet hostel or are particularly fussy about dorm privacy. Some recent reviews mention noise, warm rooms and curtains that don't fully close around the beds.

  • Area: Ciutat Vella / La Xerea
  • Nearest metro: Colón (L3, L5, L7, L9)
  • Address: Plaça de Tetuan, 5, Ciutat Vella, 46003 València
  • Website: Official Purple Nest website / direct bookings

Cantagua Hostel
Best hostel for solo travellers

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.6 / 10 (576 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 9.0 / 10 (1,073 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.6 / 5 Stars (376 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Cantagua's excellent ratings reflect its friendly staff, welcoming atmosphere and well-maintained communal spaces. Hostelworld guests particularly praise the relaxed social environment and the ease of meeting other travellers. It isn't a party hostel: quiet hours are enforced, and the common area closes at night. Recent reviews do mention that air conditioning can be inconsistent, so that's worth bearing in mind during Valencia's hotter months.

Best for: Solo travellers who want to meet people in a friendly, relaxed hostel, with the option of a female-only dorm.

Cantagua is a small, eco-friendly hostel near Ruzafa, one of Valencia's most interesting neighbourhoods for cafés, restaurants, independent shops and nightlife. It has mixed dormitories and a female-only dorm, with privacy curtains on the beds, a communal kitchen, library and comfortable shared areas. The hostel organises activities during the week, and guests often use its social atmosphere as a starting point for exploring the city together.

Not for you if: You want a party hostel or expect to be able to make noise late into the night. Cantagua has a deliberately relaxed atmosphere, with quiet hours in the dormitories after 10pm and no parties on the premises.

River Hostel
Best social hostel with a bar

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.2 / 10 (6,544 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 8.7 / 10 (5,789 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.0 / 5 Stars (1,927 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: River Hostel's consistently strong scores reflect its excellent location, friendly staff and useful facilities. Guests particularly like the communal kitchen, bar and organised activities. Its Hostelworld atmosphere score is a little lower than its location and staff scores, suggesting that it's a sociable hostel rather than a dedicated party venue. Some reviews mention occasional noise and maintenance issues, so don't expect a boutique experience.

Best for: Backpackers who want a well-equipped hostel with a bar, a big kitchen, organised activities and plenty of opportunities to meet people.

River Hostel is beside the Turia Gardens, around five minutes' walk from Valencia Cathedral and the Alameda metro station. Its common areas include a bar, a chill-out space and a projector area for watching sport and playing games. The hostel has a large fully equipped kitchen, 24-hour reception and dorm beds with curtains, reading lights, power sockets and individual storage. Activities include walking tours, pub crawls, game nights and even paella courses.

Not for you if: You want a very quiet, small hostel or expect everything to feel brand new. River is a busy, well-used backpacker hostel, and some recent guests have raised concerns about noise and the condition of particular facilities.

  • Area: Ciutat Vella, beside the Turia Gardens
  • Nearest metro: Alameda (L3, L5, L7, L9)

The Venue Hostel Boutique
Best hostel for paella nights and nightlife

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.0 / 10 (2,699 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 8.7 / 10 (593 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.2 / 5 Stars (663 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: The Venue's strong ratings reflect its friendly staff, attractive building and garden, comfortable rooms and organised social events. Hostelworld guests particularly like the atmosphere and the opportunity to meet other travellers. Its location is further from the main historic centre than most of the other hostels on this list, but the nearby metro makes getting around straightforward.

Best for: Travellers who want a characterful hostel with a garden, bar, social events, paella nights and organised pub crawls.

The Venue occupies a restored early-20th-century building in Valencia's Jesús neighbourhood. Its big selling point is the combination of historic character and modern hostel facilities: there's a garden, terrace, bar and café, coworking space, shared rooms and private suites. The hostel also runs social events, including paella evenings and pub crawls, making it a good option if you want to meet people without staying in the busiest part of the city. Joaquín Sorolla station and the Jesús metro station are both nearby.

Not for you if: You want to walk straight out of the hostel into the old town's main nightlife. The Venue is a little removed from the historic centre, and the atmosphere is more boutique and sociable than full-on party hostel.

The Central House Valencia Mercado Central
Best modern boutique hostel in the Old Town

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.4 / 10 (524 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 8.8 / 10 (337 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.4 / 5 Stars (122 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: The Central House has made a strong start since opening in 2025. Its ratings reflect its excellent old-town location, modern facilities, clean rooms and helpful staff. Guests particularly praise the comfortable beds and central position. Its social atmosphere is good, but it is a more relaxed boutique-style hostel than a dedicated party venue.

Best for: Backpackers who want modern facilities, a central location and a little more comfort without giving up the communal hostel experience.

The Central House Valencia Mercado Central is a modern boutique hostel in the heart of the old town, close to the Central Market, La Lonja and Valencia Cathedral. It opened in October 2025 and offers shared dormitories, female-only dorms and private rooms, along with a café, lounge, games room, coworking space and 24-hour reception. The location is particularly convenient if you want to explore the historic centre on foot, with public transport connections for the beach and City of Arts and Sciences.

Not for you if: You want the cheapest possible dorm bed or a hostel where the main attraction is a nightly party. The Central House is more polished and relaxed, and its newer facilities come at a price.

Where to Stay in Valencia for Nightlife

Ciutat Vella and El Carmen are the easiest choices if you want to be close to Valencia's main sights, restaurants, bars and historic streets. Most of the city's best-known backpacker hostels are here, including Hôme Youth, Purple Nest, River Hostel and The Central House. It's the most convenient area if you're visiting for the first time.

Ruzafa (Russafa) is the neighbourhood to choose if you prefer independent bars, cafés, restaurants and a more local nightlife scene. It's popular with younger Valencians and has plenty of places to eat and drink without the concentration of tourist bars found in some parts of the old town. Cantagua Hostel is just outside the neighbourhood.

Jesús is a more residential area, close to Joaquín Sorolla railway station and well connected to the rest of the city by metro. The Venue Hostel is here. It's a good choice if you like the idea of a garden, terrace and social hostel atmosphere but don't need to be in the middle of the tourist centre.

El Cabanyal and Malvarrosa are the areas to consider if beach time is your priority. They're further from the historic centre, but you'll have easier access to the sand, seafront restaurants and beach bars. The trade-off is that you'll spend more time travelling to the old town and its main nightlife areas.

How Much Do Valencia Hostels Cost?

Dorm beds in Valencia's better-known backpacker hostels generally start at around €18–€27 per night, depending on the hostel, room size, season and how far ahead you book. Private rooms cost considerably more, particularly in the historic centre.

Prices rise during Las Fallas in March, the summer beach season and busy weekends. If you're visiting during the Fallas festival, book well ahead: the city gets extremely busy and accommodation prices can climb sharply.

Valencia is generally cheaper than Barcelona and Madrid, particularly when it comes to food and going out. Staying in a hostel with a kitchen is an easy way to keep costs down, while the city's compact centre means you can do a lot of sightseeing without paying for transport.

Valencia Hostel FAQs

What is the best hostel in Valencia?

Hôme Youth Hostel is our best overall choice. It has an excellent Hostelworld rating, a central location opposite La Lonja, and a sociable atmosphere that makes it particularly good for solo backpackers.

What is the best party hostel in Valencia?

Purple Nest Hostel is our pick for party-focused backpackers. It organises regular pub crawls, social evenings and activities, and is within easy walking distance of the old town's bars and nightlife.

What is the best hostel in Valencia for solo travellers?

Hôme Youth Hostel is a strong choice if you want organised activities and a central location. Cantagua Hostel is a good alternative if you prefer a smaller, more relaxed atmosphere and the option of a female-only dorm.

What is the best area to stay in Valencia for backpackers?

Ciutat Vella is the most convenient base for first-time visitors, with most of the main sights and several popular hostels within walking distance. Ruzafa is better for independent bars, restaurants and local nightlife, while Malvarrosa and El Cabanyal are the obvious choices if beach access matters most.

How much is a hostel in Valencia?

Expect to pay roughly €18–€27 per night for a dorm bed in one of the better-known backpacker hostels, although prices vary with demand. Las Fallas, summer and busy weekends can be considerably more expensive.

Are Valencia hostels good for meeting people?

Yes. Several hostels organise walking tours, group dinners, pub crawls and other social activities. Hôme Youth, Purple Nest and River Hostel are particularly good places to start if meeting other backpackers is high on your list.

Is Valencia safe for backpackers?

Valencia is generally considered one of Spain's more relaxed major cities. Normal precautions still apply: use hostel lockers, keep an eye on your belongings in busy tourist areas and be particularly aware of pickpockets during large events such as Las Fallas.

When should I book a hostel in Valencia?

Book early if you're visiting during Las Fallas in March, the summer beach season or a major festival weekend. Outside those periods, Valencia is generally easier to book than Barcelona, but the most popular social hostels can still fill up.
