The Best Hostels in Málaga

Hostelworld ratings and review counts checked: 13 November 2026. Booking.com scores, facilities and contact details checked against current listings and official hostel websites where available.

Málaga is more than a convenient stop on the way to somewhere else. Picasso's birthplace has a handsome old centre, a proper food-and-drink scene, museums, beaches and a lively student population. It is also a useful transport hub for exploring the Costa del Sol and the rest of Andalusia. The hostel scene has evolved too: alongside traditional backpacker places, you'll find stylish rooftop hostels, private sleeping pods and newer, design-led properties.

For an all-round stay, Coeo Hernán Ruiz is a standout, with a rooftop pool, restaurant and a very strong current guest score. The Urban Jungle has one of the city's best-established social programmes and a large review history. The Lights is a more overtly social choice, while Coeo Pod Hostel Beatas is designed around individual sleeping pods. Funker and Wostel are newer options with smaller review samples; Literal is a capsule-style stay aimed at people who value privacy and a good night's sleep.

This is a shortlist, not a directory of every place to sleep in the city. For more accommodation choices and the wider city guide, see our Málaga backpacking guide and Andalusia hostel directory.

How we chose these hostels

We looked at current Hostelworld guest ratings and review volumes, then checked each property's location, facilities, social programme and official contact information. Review counts matter: a 9.3 based on 63 reviews is encouraging, but it is not as well established as a score supported by several thousand stays. We have not treated booking-platform rankings as an independent quality certification.

About the Vibe Meters: these are BackpackersBible editorial assessments of each hostel's likely strengths, based on its published facilities, activities and accommodation style. They are not guest-review scores or independently measured statistics.

The Best Backpacker Hostels in Málaga

Málaga has plenty of social hostels, but they are not all the same. Some organise dinners and pub crawls; others are more like compact capsule hotels with a shared lounge. Think about whether you want to meet people, party, work remotely or simply sleep well before choosing.

Coeo Hernán Ruiz Rooftop Pool Hostel
Best all-round hostel in Málaga

  • Booking.com Rating: 9 / 10 (3,235 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 9.5 / 10 (557 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.7 / 5 Stars (690 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Coeo Hernán Ruiz currently has one of the strongest Hostelworld scores in Málaga, with a review sample that is growing quickly. Its Hostelworld category scores include 9.8 for location and security, and 9.6 for staff. Booking.com guests also rate it highly for cleanliness and location.

Best for: Travellers who want a comfortable, modern hostel with a pool, restaurant and built-in opportunities to socialise.

This is a design-led hostel in the historic centre, with dorms, private pods and private en-suite rooms. The rooftop pool and lounge are the headline attractions, while the ground-floor restaurant and bar make it easy to eat and meet people without heading out. Dorm beds have privacy shutters, lockers and charging points. The property lists 24-hour reception and no curfew.

Not for you if: you are looking for a bargain-basement dorm. Its facilities and location come at a price, and the hostel's dorm and pod accommodation is adults-only.

  • Location: 0.8 km from the city centre.

The Urban Jungle Rooftop Hostel
Best established social hostel

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.6 / 10 (716 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 9.2 / 10 (3,072 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.4 / 5 Stars (464 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: The Urban Jungle's 9.2 is backed by one of the largest review samples among Málaga's hostels. Hostelworld scores it particularly highly for location (9.7), security (9.5), cleanliness (9.4) and staff (9.4). Its atmosphere score is 8.9: social, but not necessarily a non-stop party.

Best for: Solo travellers and backpackers who want to meet people through daily activities, rooftop drinks and shared experiences.

This small boutique hostel is in the historic centre, with a rooftop terrace and bar, a fully equipped kitchen and a coworking space. Its published activities include free walking tours, beach volleyball, yoga, dinners, happy hour and pub crawls. Dorms have privacy curtains, reading lights, sockets and lockers; private rooms have their own bathrooms. The hostel also provides free tea, coffee and fruit.

Not for you if: you are a very light sleeper. Its central location is excellent for exploring, but recent guest comments mention street noise from nearby bars.

Coeo Private Pod Hostel Beatas
Best pod-style hostel

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.7 / 10 (3,804 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 9.5 / 10 (485 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.6 / 5 Stars (995 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Coeo Beatas has an excellent Hostelworld score, and Booking.com guests particularly praise its location (9.7) and staff (9.5). The Booking.com review pool is much larger than the Hostelworld one, giving a useful second perspective.

Best for: Solo travellers who like the social side of hostels but want more privacy than a conventional bunk bed offers.

Coeo Beatas describes itself as Andalusia's first pod-style hostel. Its individual pods have air-conditioning, smart locks, a desk, wardrobe, charging points and high-speed Wi-Fi. There are single and double pods, as well as a private family room. Shared bathrooms and a communal lounge keep the hostel element, while the group also promotes walking tours, happy hours, beach volleyball and yoga activities. Free coffee, towels and luggage storage are listed as perks.

Not for you if: you want a conventional dorm room with a large shared social space, or you are travelling with children. The property lists an age restriction.

The Lights Hostel

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.5 / 10 (3,065 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 8.7 / 10 (5,105 reviews)

Best for: Solo travellers and sociable backpackers who want a central base and organised ways to meet other guests.

The Lights is beside the Atarazanas market and within easy walking distance of the main shopping streets, museums, Soho and the port. Its own website lists a rooftop terrace and bar, guest kitchen, breakfast, daily activities, themed dinners, free sangria and luggage storage. The hostel advertises walking tours, tapas and market outings, beach meet-ups and pub crawls. It has 24-hour reception.

Not for you if: you want a quiet, low-key guesthouse. Its social programme and central location are part of the attraction, but can mean noise and activity.

Booking.com currently shows a higher score from a much larger pool of reviews. Guests particularly praise the staff, cleanliness and facilities.

Best for: Travellers who like modern facilities, shared spaces and organised activities, but do not need to be right in the old town.

Reception hours are published as 08:00–23:00, with 24-hour check-in arrangements.

Not for you if: you want to step straight out into the historic centre. It is walkable, but farther from the main sights than the central hostels above.

San Andrés beach is also within walking distance. This is a practical location for transport connections, but it is not in the middle of the old town's nightlife and sightseeing area.

Not for you if: you want a big traditional backpacker dorm or a hostel right in the historic centre. Confirm the exact room configuration before booking.

Literal Hostel

Its strongest appeal is the capsule-style sleeping space and central location. Booking.com guests rate it 8.5, with particularly strong feedback for location and cleanliness.

Best for: Travellers who value a private sleeping space, clean modern facilities and a central location more than a traditional hostel atmosphere.

Literal is a capsule-style property rather than a classic backpacker hostel. Its individual sleeping cabins are designed to provide privacy within shared rooms, with personal lighting, ventilation, charging and storage. The property has a shared kitchen listed by Hostelworld, a 24-hour reception and a café. Its official website emphasises quiet, private sleeping spaces and online check-in.

Not for you if: meeting other backpackers in a large common room is a priority. Literal's appeal is closer to a compact capsule hotel than a lively social hostel.

Where to Stay in Málaga

Centro Histórico is the easiest base for a first visit. The Cathedral, Picasso Museum, Alcazaba, main shopping streets and a huge choice of tapas bars are all within walking distance. Coeo Hernán Ruiz, Coeo Beatas, The Urban Jungle and The Lights are all in or close to this area.

Soho sits just south-west of the historic centre, between the old town and the port. It is known for street art, galleries, independent cafés and restaurants. Literal is on Calle Alemania, on the edge of this central area.

María Zambrano / Carretera de Cádiz is the practical choice if you are travelling by train or bus. Wostel is near the station, with San Andrés beach also within reach. It is less atmospheric than the old town, but useful for transport connections.

La Malagueta is the best-known city beach area, east of the port. It is a pleasant walk from the centre, but do not assume a hostel described as central is right on the beach. Check the map and walking distance before booking.

Pedregalejo and El Palo, farther east along the coast, have a more local seaside feel and traditional seafood restaurants. They are worth exploring, although they are less convenient if you want to walk to the main historic sights every day.

How Much Do Málaga Hostels Cost?

The current Hostelworld listings for the hostels featured here show dorm prices starting at roughly €20–€35 per night at several properties, with some pod-style accommodation costing more. These are indicative starting prices, not a promise of what you will pay: dates, room type, demand and availability can change the rate considerably. Wostel currently shows no dorm beds available on Hostelworld.

Málaga can be more expensive than some inland Andalusian cities, particularly during summer, Easter and major event periods. The city is also a popular weekend destination, so Friday and Saturday nights can cost more than midweek stays.

Compare like with like. A pod with more privacy may cost more than a standard dorm bed, while a private room at a hostel can sometimes compete with a budget hotel. Check whether breakfast, towels and luggage storage are included, and whether the quoted rate is refundable.

Málaga Hostel FAQs

What is the best hostel in Málaga?

Coeo Hernán Ruiz Rooftop Pool Hostel is our best all-round choice because it combines a strong current guest score with a rooftop pool, restaurant, central location and 24-hour reception. The Urban Jungle is a strong alternative if social activities and a well-established review history matter more to you.

What is the best hostel in Málaga for solo travellers?

The Urban Jungle and The Lights are good choices for solo travellers who want organised activities and a social atmosphere. Coeo Beatas is worth considering if you prefer the privacy of a pod while still having access to shared spaces and activities.

Which Málaga hostel is best for partying?

The Lights and The Urban Jungle both advertise regular social events, rooftop drinks and pub crawls. Coeo Hernán Ruiz also has a rooftop pool, bar and organised events. These are social hostels rather than guarantees of a wild party every night; check the current programme before booking.

What is the best area to stay in Málaga?

For a first visit, the Centro Histórico is the most convenient base for sightseeing, restaurants and nightlife. Soho is a good alternative if you like independent cafés and street art. Stay near María Zambrano station if transport connections are your main priority.

Are Málaga hostels suitable for solo female travellers?

Several properties offer private pods or female-only room options, but room configurations and policies can change. Coeo Beatas lists pod accommodation and female pod options; check the room type carefully when booking. Use the lockers provided and read recent reviews, particularly for comments about room security and shared bathrooms.

Do Málaga hostels have kitchens?

Some do. The Urban Jungle, The Lights and Funker list shared kitchens, while Literal lists a shared kitchen on Hostelworld. Coeo's properties have their own mix of lounges, cafés and food services, so check the specific property rather than assuming all Coeo locations have the same facilities.

How much is a hostel in Málaga?

As a rough guide, dorm beds at the featured properties are currently advertised from around €20–€35 a night, although pods and peak dates can cost more. Prices change frequently, so compare your actual dates and the room type before making a decision.

Is Málaga walkable?

The historic centre is compact and easy to explore on foot. The port and La Malagueta beach are also walkable from the centre, although the train station and more distant beaches may involve a longer walk or a bus ride. Check the exact location of your hostel rather than relying on the word "central".

When should I book a hostel in Málaga?

Book early for summer, Easter, long weekends and major local events such as the August Feria de Málaga. For quieter dates you may find availability closer to arrival, but compare cancellation conditions and check-in arrangements before committing.
