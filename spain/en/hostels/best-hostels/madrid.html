The Best Hostels in Madrid

Madrid is Spain’s best city for backpackers who want late nights, cheap tapas, easy airport access and a hostel scene where making friends is part of the experience. The best hostel for you depends less on star ratings and more on one question: what kind of fun are you here for?

If you want to party until sunrise, Cats Party Hostel is the obvious choice. If you are travelling alone and want a ready-made group, choose Onefam Sungate. If you want a rooftop terrace, good design and a slightly more grown-up hostel stay, book The Hat Madrid.

This guide covers the best party hostels, social hostels, budget picks and comfort stays in Madrid, with neighbourhood advice, typical prices, booking tips and direct hostel contact details. For the complete accommodation directory, see our full Madrid backpacking guide.

  • Best party hostel: Cats Party Hostel
  • Best for solo travellers: Onefam Sungate
  • Best hostel with a rooftop terrace: The Hat Madrid
  • Best hostel with a pool: The Central House Lavapiés
  • Best hostel for female solo travellers: Safestay Madrid
  • Typical dorm price: €12–€25 per night

Best Party Hostels in Madrid

Madrid’s party hostels are built for backpackers who want pre-drinks in a courtyard, a pub crawl with new friends, and an alarm clock that is technically optional. Here are the best ones, ranked by the type of night out they create.

Cats Party Hostel — Best overall party hostel

Best for: Backpackers who want Madrid’s biggest, loudest and most social hostel experience.

Cats Party Hostel occupies a 17th-century Spanish palace near La Latina, with an Andalusian-style patio, a bar that brews its own beer, a cinema room, live music, karaoke, dance lessons, flamenco nights and themed parties. With 153 beds, there is always a group forming.

The biggest draw is the nightly pub crawl, plus a weekend paella party. If you want to arrive alone and leave with a crew, this is the hostel to book.

Not for you if: you want early nights, quiet dorms, or a design-led boutique hostel. This is a party hostel first.

  • Area: Between Puerta del Sol and La Latina
  • Nearest metro: Lavapiés (L3) / Tirso de Molina (L1)
  • Address: Calle de los Cañizares, 6, 28012 Madrid
  • Website: Official Cats Hostel Madrid Sol website / direct bookings
  • Phone: +34 913 69 28 07
  • Email: madridsol@catshostels.com

Onefam Sungate — Best hostel for solo travellers

Best for: First-time solo backpackers who want a smaller hostel with a built-in social group.

Onefam Sungate is one of Madrid’s best hostels for solo travellers because it removes the usual hostel barriers: there are no bunk beds, rooms feel more like a simple hotel, and the staff actively bring guests together. Free group dinners, daily activities, pub crawls and a shared kitchen make it easy to meet people.

It is only about three minutes from Puerta del Sol, placing you in the middle of Madrid’s old centre, shopping streets, tapas bars and nightlife.

Not for you if: you want a huge party hostel with hundreds of guests. Cats Party Hostel will give you a bigger crowd.

  • Area: Centro, about three minutes from Puerta del Sol
  • Nearest metro: Sol (L1, L2, L3)

The Hat Madrid — Best hostel with a rooftop terrace

Best for: Backpackers who want a social hostel with a more stylish, grown-up atmosphere.

The Hat Madrid sits directly on Plaza Mayor, one of the best locations in the city. Its rooftop terrace is ideal for sunset drinks, meeting other travellers and occasionally catching live music. The hostel also offers free walking tours, bike rental and a well-regarded breakfast buffet.

This is not a hardcore party hostel, but it is highly social. After drinks on the roof, staff can point you towards La Latina, Huertas, Malasaña or Chueca for a proper Madrid night out.

Not for you if: you want a kitchen—The Hat does not have one—or you want the wildest possible hostel party.

  • Area: Plaza Mayor
  • Nearest metro: Sol (L1, L2, L3) / Ópera (L2, L5, R)
  • Address: C. Imperial, 9, Centro, 28012 Madrid

The Central House Lavapiés — Best hostel with a pool

Best for: Travellers who want design, comfort, activities and a rooftop pool without leaving the hostel scene behind.

The Central House Lavapiés feels closer to a design hotel than a traditional backpacker hostel. It has a rooftop pool, terrace, outdoor patio, bar, restaurant, VR and console games room, and daily activities including gallery tours, cinema outings, cooking classes and pub crawls.

It is in Lavapiés, one of Madrid’s most interesting neighbourhoods: multicultural, full of street art, cafés and bars, and within walking distance of El Rastro, Puerta del Sol and the Reina Sofía Museum.

Not for you if: you want the cheapest possible bed or a hostel built entirely around drinking. It is social, but more polished than Cats or OK Hostel.

  • Area: Lavapiés
  • Nearest metro: Lavapiés (L3)

Safestay Madrid — Best hostel for female solo travellers

Best for: Female solo travellers who want security, comfort and a social atmosphere without sleeping in the middle of the loudest nightlife zone.

Safestay Madrid has female-only dorms, card-access rooms, 24-hour reception, quiet workspaces, a cinema and a rooftop terrace with a bar and city views. It is near Alonso Martínez, within walking distance of Chueca, Malasaña and the Fuencarral area.

It is social rather than wild: expect game nights, pizza parties, a rooftop crowd and an easy base for exploring Madrid’s nightlife without living directly above it.

Not for you if: you want to be in the exact centre of the bar scene. It is close, but slightly north of Sol.

  • Area: Near Alonso Martínez / Chueca
  • Nearest metro: Alonso Martínez (L4, L5, L10)

OK Hostel Madrid — Best hostel for groups

Best for: Groups who want a fun, affordable base in La Latina with plenty of space to hang out together.

OK Hostel Madrid is designed around socialising: there is a bar, restaurant, common area, board games, pool table and gaming consoles. The hostel runs pub crawls, social dinners and tapas tours, and its La Latina location puts you near classic Madrid tapas bars.

A major bonus is El Rastro, Madrid’s famous Sunday flea market, which takes place directly outside the hostel. Check out on a Sunday morning and you walk straight into it.

Not for you if: you want a sleek design hostel or a quiet, boutique-style stay.

  • Area: La Latina
  • Nearest metro: La Latina (L5)
  • Address: C. de Juanelo, 24, Centro, 28012 Madrid

Room 007 Ventura — Best hostel for a smarter night out

Best for: Travellers who want wine on a roof terrace before heading to clubs, rather than drinking games in the common room.

Room 007 Ventura is near Barrio de las Letras, one of Madrid’s best nightlife areas. It has a roof terrace popular for wine and food before guests head out, and staff often lead pub crawls and club hops, sometimes jointly with other Madrid hostels.

The crowd tends to be slightly older and more relaxed than at Cats, but the hostel is still genuinely social and well placed for bars and clubs.

Not for you if: you want the loudest, youngest party-hostel atmosphere. Cats Party Hostel is the better choice for that.

  • Area: Centro / edge of Barrio de las Letras
  • Nearest metro: Sevilla (L2) / Sol (L1, L2, L3)

Where to Stay in Madrid for Nightlife

Sol and Plaza Mayor are best for first-time visitors who want to walk everywhere. It is central, busy and touristy, with easy access to La Latina, Huertas and Malasaña. The Hat, Cats and Onefam Sungate are all strong choices nearby.

La Latina is best for tapas, vermouth, Sunday El Rastro and a more classic Spanish bar scene. Cats Party Hostel and OK Hostel Madrid are both excellent bases here.

Malasaña is best for a younger, alternative crowd: indie bars, live music, cheap drinks and a more local-feeling night out. Safestay is conveniently placed nearby.

Chueca is best for LGBTQ+ nightlife and one of Madrid’s most energetic weekend scenes. Safestay and Room007 Chueca are practical nearby options.

Huertas / Barrio de las Letras is best for the highest concentration of bars, clubs and late-night streets. Room 007 Ventura is the natural hostel choice.

How Much Do Hostels in Madrid Cost?

Most dorm beds in Madrid cost around €12–€25 per night, with central and design-led hostels often closer to €18–€30. Private rooms generally start around €55–€95, although prices vary sharply by date.

Madrid has no tourist tax, which makes it easier to compare hostel prices than in Barcelona. Weekends and Real Madrid home games can push central hostel prices up by around 20%.

For a three-night stay, a realistic backpacker budget is around €150–€250 including a dorm bed, food, drinks, transport and a couple of nights out.

Madrid Hostel FAQs

What is the best party hostel in Madrid?

Cats Party Hostel is the best party hostel in Madrid for most backpackers. It has a nightly pub crawl, a bar, regular events, a weekend paella party and a large social crowd.

What is the best hostel in Madrid for solo travellers?

Onefam Sungate is the best hostel for solo travellers. It has no bunk beds, free group dinners, daily activities and a welcoming, social atmosphere.

What is the best area to stay in Madrid for backpackers?

Stay between Puerta del Sol, Plaza Mayor and La Latina if you want the best all-round base. It puts the main sights, tapas bars and nightlife within walking distance.

How much is a hostel in Madrid?

Expect roughly €12–€25 per night for a dorm bed, with higher prices in high season, at weekends and during major events.

Is there a tourist tax in Madrid?

No. Madrid does not currently charge a city or regional tourist tax, so the rate you book is generally the rate you pay.

Are hostels in Madrid safe?

Yes. Madrid is one of Europe’s safer capitals, and most hostels have lockers and 24-hour reception. The main issue is pickpocketing around Sol, Gran Vía and crowded public transport.

When should I book a hostel in Madrid?

Book early for San Isidro in mid-May and Pride in late June/early July, when Chueca becomes one of Europe’s biggest street parties and beds sell out quickly.
