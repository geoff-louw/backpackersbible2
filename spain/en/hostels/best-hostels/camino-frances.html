The Best Hostels Along the Camino Francés
(Last 100km)

Hostel information, ratings and prices checked: 13 November 2026.

The Camino Francés is Spain's classic pilgrimage route, and the final stretch from Sarria to Santiago de Compostela is one of the most popular sections of the entire Camino network. It's also where the accommodation starts getting particularly interesting. Alongside traditional pilgrim albergues, you'll find converted farmhouses, sociable hostels, country guesthouses, gardens, swimming pools and some wonderfully quirky places to spend the night.

The trick is knowing where to stop. Some of the best places aren't necessarily in the obvious overnight towns. A handful of small rural albergues between Palas de Rei, Melide and Arzúa have developed particularly loyal followings among experienced Camino walkers, often because of their communal dinners, welcoming owners and relaxed atmosphere.

If you're travelling independently, Casa Barbadelo is a particularly interesting first stop, Casa Domingo is famous for its communal dinners, and Taberna Vella (Heidi's Place) offers a much more intimate experience than the larger pilgrim hostels. For something more conventional, Albergue O Candil in Melide and Albergue PortoSantiago in Portomarín are good options.

This guide focuses on the best accommodation for younger independent travellers, backpackers and sociable pilgrims along the Camino Francés from Sarria to Santiago. We've included places worth making a detour for, as well as practical accommodation at the main overnight stops. For the complete accommodation directory, see our full Camino Francés hostel directory.

How we chose these hostels

We compared accommodation along the Camino Francés using guest ratings on Booking.com, Google and Hostelworld, where available, together with location, cleanliness, facilities, atmosphere, value for money and direct booking options. We also considered recommendations from experienced Camino walkers, particularly for smaller rural albergues that don't necessarily appear prominently on the major booking platforms.

Unlike a conventional backpacker city, the Camino has a mixture of private albergues, public pilgrim hostels and small family-run guesthouses. Some of the most memorable places have only a handful of beds, while others offer swimming pools, restaurants and large communal areas. We've tried to include a variety of experiences rather than simply choosing the biggest properties or those with the highest online ratings.

Most public albergues operate on a first-come, first-served basis and do not accept advance reservations. Private albergues generally accept bookings, although some smaller establishments only take reservations a few days ahead. Always check opening dates and booking arrangements directly with the property.

Best Hostels Along the Camino Francés

The accommodation below is arranged in walking order, starting around Sarria and finishing on the approach to Santiago. We've included both conventional overnight stops and a few smaller places that are worth considering if you're happy to adjust your walking stages.

Casa Barbadelo
Best hostel with a swimming pool

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.8 / 10 (1,372 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.4 / 5 Stars (1,182 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Casa Barbadelo has developed a particularly loyal following among Camino walkers, with repeated recommendations for its swimming pool, friendly atmosphere and relaxed rural setting. Its appeal is as much about the experience as the accommodation itself. It's a larger establishment than some of the little family-run albergues further along the route, so don't expect an intimate guesthouse atmosphere.

Best for: Backpackers who enjoy outdoor social spaces, swimming pools, communal meals and a little countryside relaxation.

Casa Barbadelo is in Vilei, approximately 3.6 km beyond Sarria on the Camino. Its swimming pool is a major attraction, particularly during the warmer months, while the property also has a restaurant, garden and a mixture of dormitory and private accommodation.

One interesting option is to arrive in Sarria, collect your bearings and walk the extra few kilometres to Casa Barbadelo that afternoon. This gives you a head start on the following morning's walk and means you can avoid some of the crowds leaving Sarria. It's also a nice way to begin the Camino somewhere a little more rural than the town itself.

Not for you if: You want to stay in the centre of Sarria or prefer a tiny, quiet family-run albergue. It's also worth checking that the swimming pool is open if that's your main reason for choosing it.

  • Area: Vilei / Barbadelo, outside Sarria
  • Camino stage: Sarria to Portomarín
  • Address: Vilei, 27616 Sarria, Lugo

A Casa de Carmen
Best homely rural albergue

  • Booking.com Rating: 9.8 / 10 (61 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.4 / 5 Stars (322 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: A Casa de Carmen is one of those small Camino establishments that seems to inspire particularly affectionate recommendations. Guests and fellow pilgrims repeatedly mention its welcoming owners, communal atmosphere and countryside setting. With only around twenty beds, it offers a much more personal experience than the large albergues in the main towns.

Best for: Solo travellers and couples who enjoy meeting people around a communal dinner table rather than staying in a large, anonymous dormitory.

A Casa de Carmen is in O Mosteiro, in the Barbadelo area, approximately 4.2 km beyond Sarria. It's run by an Italian couple and has a lovely rural setting, with horses, outdoor space and somewhere to enjoy the sunset. One of its attractions is the communal dinner, usually served around 18:30, which gives guests an opportunity to get to know one another.

It's a little different from the conventional pilgrim hostel experience. Rather than simply providing somewhere to sleep, the owners have created a welcoming, homely atmosphere that makes it feel more like staying with friends in the countryside.

Not for you if: You want a large hostel with lots of facilities, nightlife or the convenience of staying in Sarria itself. This is a small rural establishment, and its communal atmosphere is part of the experience.

  • Camino stage: Sarria to Portomarín

Albergue PortoSantiago
Best hostel in Portomarín

    • Booking.com Rating: 9.9 / 10 (1,672 reviews)
    • Google Places Rating: 4.6 / 5 Stars (435 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: PortoSantiago has a strong Google rating and a good reputation for cleanliness, comfort and attentive hosting. Guests appreciate the organised facilities, comfortable bedding and practical extras, making it a particularly good option if you want something a little more comfortable than a basic communal pilgrim albergue.

Best for: Backpackers who want a clean, comfortable and well-equipped hostel in one of the Camino's main overnight towns.

PortoSantiago is a privately run albergue and guesthouse in central Portomarín. It offers shared accommodation alongside more private room options, with comfortable bedding, practical facilities and easy access to the town's restaurants, cafés and shops.

Portomarín is a particularly popular overnight stop, and the town has a good selection of places to eat and drink. PortoSantiago is well positioned for exploring the centre and meeting other pilgrims after a day's walking.

Not for you if: You want a big party hostel with organised nightlife. PortoSantiago is more about comfort and a good night's rest than entertainment.

Albergue Casa Domingo
Best communal dinner experience

  • Booking.com Rating: 9 / 10 (320 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.5 / 5 Stars (686 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Casa Domingo is one of the most frequently recommended rural albergues on this stretch of the Camino. Its appeal is less about luxury facilities and more about the experience of staying in an old farmhouse with welcoming hosts and a memorable communal dinner. It has a particularly good reputation for catering to vegetarians and vegans.

Best for: Solo backpackers, food lovers and anyone who enjoys the social side of the Camino. If you want to meet fellow travellers over a home-cooked meal, this is a particularly good choice.

Casa Domingo is in Ponte Campaña, a rural settlement beyond Palas de Rei, between San Xulián and Casanova. It's an old farmhouse, with former stables converted into dormitory accommodation. The property has around 21 beds, giving it a much more intimate atmosphere than the larger town hostels.

The communal dinner is the big attraction. Guests regularly recommend the food and the opportunity to sit down together after a long day on the trail. It's also particularly appreciated by vegetarians and vegans, who can sometimes find their options limited in traditional pilgrim restaurants.

Casa Domingo is a good example of why it's worth looking beyond the obvious overnight towns. If you're prepared to adjust your walking distance, you can find some of the most memorable accommodation experiences in the smaller villages.

Not for you if: You prefer modern facilities, lots of privacy or the convenience of staying in the centre of Palas de Rei. This is a small rural albergue, and you'll need to book ahead during busy periods.

  • Area: Ponte Campaña, beyond Palas de Rei
  • Camino stage: Portomarín to Palas de Rei / Palas de Rei to Arzúa
  • Address: Lugar Ponte Campaña, 27204 Lugo

Albergue O Candil
Best hostel in Melide

  • Booking.com Rating: 9.4 / 10 (1,109 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.7 / 5 Stars (300 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: O Candil's excellent Booking.com score, supported by a substantial number of reviews, makes it one of the more convincing accommodation choices along this section of the Camino. Guests particularly praise the friendly owners, cleanliness and comfortable facilities. Reviews also highlight its smaller dormitories, which offer a little more privacy than the large communal sleeping rooms found in some traditional albergues.

Best for: Backpackers who want a comfortable, sociable stop in Melide, with friendly hosts and good facilities.

Albergue O Candil is a well-regarded private albergue in the centre of Melide. It has relatively small dormitory rooms, a communal garden and comfortable shared facilities. The atmosphere is friendly and relaxed, and guests frequently mention the helpful owners.

Melide is a particularly interesting stop because it's where the Camino Francés meets the Camino Primitivo. That gives the town a lively international atmosphere, with pilgrims arriving from different routes and plenty of opportunities to meet other walkers.

It's also a good place to try one of Galicia's best-known dishes: pulpo a feira, or Galician-style octopus. Melide is famous for its pulperías, and a meal here is something worth making time for.

Not for you if: You want a large hostel with a bar and organised activities. O Candil is more about comfortable accommodation and friendly hospitality than a party atmosphere.

Albergue El Alemán
Best rural hostel with a garden

  • Booking.com Rating: 9.2 / 10 (1,093 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.2 / 5 Stars (981 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: El Alemán has a strong Booking.com rating and a particularly good reputation among pilgrims who prefer to walk beyond Melide rather than stop in the town. Reviews consistently mention the garden, cleanliness, excellent showers and useful laundry facilities. Its main compromise is that the dormitories are relatively large and the number of toilets can be limited when the hostel is full.

Best for: Travellers who enjoy rural accommodation, outdoor relaxation and a friendly place to recover after a long day's walking.

El Alemán is in Boente, approximately 5.6 km beyond Melide on the way to Arzúa. It's a private pilgrim albergue with a garden, communal areas and useful facilities for walkers, including laundry and good showers. There's also a bar-restaurant nearby, making it easy to get something to eat without having to walk further.

The garden is one of its biggest attractions. After several hours on the Camino, being able to sit outside, relax and enjoy the countryside is a welcome change from the larger town hostels.

It's particularly worth considering if you don't want to stop in Melide and would rather break up the long walking stage towards Arzúa.

Not for you if: You want to stay in a town with plenty of restaurants and nightlife. El Alemán is in a small rural settlement, and it's primarily designed around the needs of pilgrims.

  • Area: Boente, between Melide and Arzúa
  • Camino stage: Palas de Rei to Arzúa
  • Address: Lugar Boente Arriba, 49, 15826 A Peroxa, A Coruña

Albergue de Ribadiso
Best scenic pilgrim hostel

What makes it special: Ribadiso isn't a conventional backpacker hostel, and that's precisely the point. It's one of the most atmospheric public pilgrim albergues on the final stretch of the Camino, thanks to its beautiful riverside setting. The stone bridge, woodland surroundings and riverbank make it a particularly memorable place to spend the night.

Best for: Backpackers who enjoy nature, swimming spots, beautiful surroundings and a more traditional Camino experience.

Albergue de Ribadiso is beside the River Iso, in Ribadiso de Baixo, a small settlement just before Arzúa. The setting is the main attraction. Pilgrims can relax beside the river, cool their feet in the water and enjoy one of the prettiest natural locations along this stretch of the route.

It's a public albergue operated by the Xunta de Galicia, rather than a privately run hostel. The indicative 2026 dormitory price is around €10 per person, making it one of the cheaper options on this list. Public albergues don't accept normal advance reservations, so beds are allocated on arrival, subject to availability.

The showers and toilets are in a separate building, which is worth knowing before you arrive. The riverside setting is so popular that this public albergue can become busy, particularly during the summer.

Not for you if: You need guaranteed accommodation, private rooms or hotel-style facilities. This is traditional public pilgrim accommodation, and you cannot book a bed in advance.

  • Area: Ribadiso de Baixo, near Arzúa
  • Camino stage: Palas de Rei to Arzúa
  • Address: Ribadiso de Baixo, 15819 Arzúa, A Coruña
  • Type: Public Xunta pilgrim albergue
  • Indicative price: Approximately €10 per bed
  • Booking: First come, first served. No advance reservations.
  • Website: Official Camino de Santiago / Xunta information
  • Email: info@albergueelaleman.com

Taberna Vella (Heidi's Place)
Best intimate hostel experience

  • Google Places Rating: 4.8 / 5 Stars (216 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Taberna Vella has one of the strongest reputations among the smaller private albergues on this stretch. Its Google rating is supported by particularly enthusiastic recommendations from Camino walkers, who repeatedly mention the home-cooked dinner, immaculate accommodation, garden and personal hospitality. With only eight beds, it offers a very different experience from the larger establishments along the route.

Best for: Solo travellers and couples who prefer intimate, home-style accommodation and enjoy getting to know their hosts and fellow pilgrims.

Taberna Vella, better known among Camino walkers as Heidi's Place, is in As Quintas, approximately 5 km beyond Arzúa. It's a tiny private albergue with just eight beds, run by Heidi, and has developed something of a cult following among people walking the Camino.

The experience is closer to staying in someone's home than a conventional hostel. Guests regularly praise the home-cooked communal dinner, the garden and the exceptionally clean accommodation. The small number of beds makes it particularly sociable, and the intimate setting is a big part of its appeal.

There are some house rules, including where backpacks can be placed, so don't expect the completely informal atmosphere of a large backpacker hostel. It's also important to book ahead: with only eight beds, turning up without a reservation is a gamble.

Not for you if: You prefer large dormitories, lots of independence or the freedom to arrive whenever you feel like it. This is a small, carefully run establishment with its own house rules.

  • Area: As Quintas / Burres, beyond Arzúa
  • Camino stage: Arzúa to O Pedrouzo
  • Address: Lugar de Taberna Vella, s/n, 15819 As Quintas, A Coruña
  • Website: Official Taberna Vella website / direct bookings
  • Phone: +34 687 54 38 10
  • Email: heidi.tasin@gmail.com

Albergue Espíritu Xacobeo
Best peaceful hostel near O Pedrouzo

  • Google Places Rating: 4.7 / 5 Stars (648 reviews)
  • Booking.com Rating: 9 / 10 (2,166 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Espíritu Xacobeo has a strong Google rating and is particularly well regarded for cleanliness, comfortable beds and its peaceful rural setting. Guests appreciate the outdoor seating, well-equipped kitchen and laundry facilities. Its location just outside the main concentration of accommodation in O Pedrouzo is a bonus if you want somewhere quieter before the final walk into Santiago.

Best for: Backpackers who want a peaceful final overnight stop, modern facilities and a little breathing space before reaching Santiago.

Albergue Espíritu Xacobeo is in A Rúa, near O Pedrouzo, in a quieter rural setting along the Camino. It offers comfortable dormitory accommodation, a garden and outdoor seating, shared kitchen facilities and laundry. Guests frequently praise the cleanliness and the attention to detail, making it a particularly appealing alternative to the larger accommodation establishments in O Pedrouzo itself.

It's a good option if you'd rather finish the day somewhere peaceful than stay in the busiest part of town. The slightly more rural atmosphere also makes it a pleasant place to prepare for the final walk to Santiago.

Not for you if: You want to stay right in the centre of O Pedrouzo, with bars and restaurants on your doorstep. This is a quieter countryside option, and you'll need to plan your evening meal accordingly.

  • Area: A Rúa / O Pedrouzo
  • Camino stage: Arzúa to O Pedrouzo / O Pedrouzo to Santiago

Where to Stay Along the Camino Francés

Sarria: The traditional starting point for the final stretch of the Camino. It's a convenient town with plenty of accommodation, supermarkets, cafés and restaurants. If you prefer a rural setting, Casa Barbadelo and A Casa de Carmen are just a few kilometres further along the route.

Portomarín: One of the most popular overnight stops, with a good selection of restaurants, bars and private albergues. It's a pleasant place to spend an evening, and Albergue PortoSantiago is a good option if you want comfortable accommodation in the town.

Palas de Rei and the surrounding villages: Palas de Rei is a convenient overnight stop, with several private albergues and traditional pilgrim accommodation. If you're looking for something more personal, Casa Domingo in Ponte Campaña is worth considering. The smaller rural establishments are often more memorable than the conventional town hostels.

Melide: A lively Camino town with a good selection of accommodation, restaurants and bars. It's where the Francés meets the Primitivo, so there's a particularly international atmosphere. Albergue O Candil is one of the better-rated options.

Boente and Ribadiso: If you're happy to break away from the conventional overnight stops, this section has some particularly attractive accommodation. El Alemán in Boente is a good private albergue, while Ribadiso offers a memorable riverside setting and a traditional public pilgrim hostel.

Arzúa and the surrounding villages: Arzúa has plenty of accommodation, restaurants and shops, making it a practical overnight stop. However, Taberna Vella in As Quintas, a few kilometres beyond town, is one of the more memorable small albergues on the route.

O Pedrouzo: The most popular final overnight stop before Santiago, with plenty of pilgrim accommodation, restaurants and cafés. If you prefer somewhere quieter, Espíritu Xacobeo in nearby A Rúa is a good alternative.

Lavacolla and Vilamaior: There are also rural accommodation options beyond O Pedrouzo for anyone who wants to split the final approach differently. Casa de Amancio and Albergue O Fervello are worth investigating. These are alternatives for walkers who want to spend their final night closer to Santiago rather than staying in the city itself.

For hostels in the historic centre of Santiago de Compostela itself, see our separate Best Hostels in Santiago de Compostela guide.

How Much Do Camino Francés Hostels Cost?

One of the advantages of walking the Camino is that accommodation can be considerably cheaper than in Spain's major backpacker cities. Traditional pilgrim albergues are particularly affordable, although private hostels and rural guesthouses can cost more.

As a rough guide, expect to pay around €10–€18 per night for a dormitory bed in a public or basic private albergue. More comfortable private albergues and smaller rural establishments typically charge around €15–€25, while private rooms and guesthouses can cost considerably more.

For example, the public albergue at Ribadiso is around €10 per bed, while the particularly popular small rural establishments such as Casa Domingo and Taberna Vella have indicative dormitory prices of approximately €15–€18.

Prices vary according to the season, facilities and availability. July and August are particularly busy, as are the Easter holidays. Some of the smaller private albergues also close during the winter months, often reopening around Easter.

It's worth carrying a little cash, particularly if you intend to stay in smaller villages or use public albergues. Not every establishment accepts cards, and some traditional pilgrim accommodation operates on a different booking system from conventional hotels.

For a comfortable budget, allow approximately €35–€55 per day for a dormitory bed, meals, snacks, drinks and other basic expenses, depending on how much you spend eating out and which accommodation you choose.

Camino Francés Hostel FAQs

What is the best hostel on the Camino Francés?

There isn't one obvious winner, as the best accommodation depends on the experience you're looking for. Casa Barbadelo is a good choice if you want a swimming pool and outdoor space, Casa Domingo is particularly good for communal meals, and Taberna Vella is one of the most memorable small albergues along the route.

What is the best hostel on the Camino for solo travellers?

Casa Domingo and Taberna Vella are particularly good choices for solo travellers who want to meet people. Their smaller size and communal atmosphere make it easier to get to know fellow pilgrims. Casa Barbadelo is another good option if you enjoy larger social spaces.

Are there party hostels along the Camino Francés?

Do I need to book Camino hostels in advance?

It's a good idea to book private albergues in advance during the busy season, particularly if you're travelling in a group or have a specific place in mind. Smaller establishments such as Taberna Vella have very limited capacity and can fill up quickly. Public Xunta albergues generally don't accept advance reservations and allocate beds on arrival.

Are public and private albergues different?

Can you stay in normal hostels rather than pilgrim albergues?

Absolutely. You don't have to stay in traditional pilgrim accommodation. There are private hostels, guesthouses, pensions and small hotels along the route. These can be particularly appealing if you want more privacy, a private bathroom or a little more comfort after a long day of walking.

When is the best time to walk the Camino Francés?

May, June and September are particularly good months, with generally pleasant walking temperatures and fewer crowds than the peak summer period. July and August are busier and can be hot, although there's usually a greater choice of open accommodation and organised services. Some rural albergues close during the winter, so check opening dates if you're walking outside the main season.
