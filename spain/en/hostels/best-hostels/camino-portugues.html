The Best Hostels on the Camino Portugués – Last 100km

Ratings and accommodation details checked: 14 November 2026.

The Camino Portugués is one of the most popular routes to Santiago de Compostela, and the stretch from Tui is the classic starting point for pilgrims walking the final part of the Portuguese Way. It is a particularly good option for first-time Camino walkers: there are plenty of places to stay, a lively international pilgrim community, and a good mix of traditional albergues and modern hostels.

There is a difference between the two. A traditional albergue is primarily designed for pilgrims: shared dormitories, practical facilities and an atmosphere shaped by the Camino. A conventional backpacker hostel is more likely to offer a social common room, a wider range of room types and a more familiar hostel experience. Some places combine the best of both.

We've picked out some of the best places to stay between Tui and Santiago, including well-rated pilgrim hostels, sociable backpacker options and a few smaller places worth making a stop for. For the complete list, including accommodation in every town and village, see our full Camino Portugués hostel directory.

How we chose these hostels

We compared accommodation along the Tui–Santiago route using Google ratings, available guest feedback, location, facilities, atmosphere and value for money. We gave preference to places that are genuinely useful to backpackers and pilgrims: welcoming hosts, clean dormitories, good communal facilities and convenient access to the Camino.

Not every good place to stay is a conventional hostel. Some of the best options are small, independently run pilgrim albergues, while others are guesthouses with private rooms. We've included both, but made the distinction clear so you know what to expect.

Our Vibe Meter scores are editorial assessments, not ratings copied from booking websites. They are intended to give you an idea of the atmosphere and facilities before you book.

Best Hostels on the Camino Portugués

These recommendations are arranged in walking order, starting in Tui. You don't have to follow the traditional stage divisions: there are plenty of smaller villages and alternative overnight stops along the way. If you want to stay somewhere quieter, or avoid the busiest pilgrim hostels, it is worth looking beyond the obvious stopping points.

A Troita Hostel
Best hostel in Tui

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.6 / 10 (619 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.8 / 5 Stars (82 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: A Troita has one of the strongest Google ratings among the Tui hostels in our directory. It is a smaller, pilgrim-focused place rather than a large, party-oriented backpacker hostel. Its quieter setting makes it an appealing choice if you want a good night's sleep before setting off.

Best for: Pilgrims who want a comfortable, modern place to stay before starting the Camino.

A Troita is a little outside Tui's historic centre, in a greener and quieter setting. That means a short walk uphill rather than stepping straight out of the front door onto the main streets, but the trade-off is a more peaceful place to rest. It is particularly worth considering if you are arriving in Tui the day before you start walking.

Not for you if: you want to be right in the middle of Tui's old town, close to its restaurants and bars. There are more central options.

  • Address: Estrada Baiona, 36700 Tui, Pontevedra

Albergue Pallanes
Best quiet pilgrim hostel in Tui

  • Google Places Rating: 4.8 / 5 Stars (538 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Pallanes is another highly rated private albergue in Tui. It is known for its distinctive manor-house setting and extensive grounds, giving it a rather different feel from the more compact hostels in the town centre.

Best for: Pilgrims who prefer a little space, a garden and a quieter atmosphere before beginning the walk.

Albergue Pallanes is set in a large property just outside the centre of Tui, but close to the Camino route. It is a good alternative to staying in the middle of town, particularly if you are looking for somewhere to relax and get organised before the first day's walk. Reservations are accepted, and booking ahead is sensible during the busier months.

Not for you if: you want a lively hostel with a bar, organised activities or lots of other travellers arriving and departing throughout the day.

The hostel offers a more intimate alternative to the larger pilgrim accommodation in the area.

Camino Portugués Hostel Directory

Where to Stay on the Camino Portugués

Tui is the traditional starting point for this stretch of the Portuguese Way. It has several private albergues as well as a public pilgrim hostel. If you are arriving the day before you start walking, it is worth staying in the historic centre so you can explore the town and get organised.

O Porriño is the usual first overnight stop for walkers following the standard stages. There are several private albergues and a public pilgrim hostel, but some accommodation is outside the town centre. Check the location before booking.

Redondela is one of the main overnight stops on the route. It has a good choice of private albergues, guesthouses and public pilgrim accommodation. It is a popular place to stop, so booking ahead is sensible in the busier months.

Pontevedra is one of the most attractive towns along the route and a particularly good place to spend an evening. There is a public pilgrim albergue, several private hostels and plenty of conventional accommodation. If you want a little more than a bed for the night, this is a good place to choose a hostel in the historic centre.

Caldas de Reis is another popular stop, famous for its thermal springs. There are plenty of accommodation options, from traditional albergues to guesthouses and hotels. It is worth booking ahead during the main walking season.

Padrón is a common overnight stop before the final walk to Santiago. It has a good selection of private albergues and a public pilgrim hostel. If you want to avoid the busiest accommodation, look at the smaller rural places beyond town.

Between Padrón and Santiago there are several rural accommodation options, including places around A Escravitude and O Faramello. These can be useful if you want to divide the final walk differently, rather than staying in Padrón and completing the whole final stage in one go.

How Much Do Camino Portugués Hostels Cost?

The cheapest accommodation is usually found in the public pilgrim albergues run by the Xunta de Galicia. These generally charge around €10 per person per night, making them excellent value for budget-conscious walkers.

Private albergues and hostels cost more, with prices depending on the town, season, facilities and room type. Dormitory beds are generally the cheapest option, while private rooms and guesthouses can cost considerably more. Check current prices directly with the accommodation before setting your budget.

Public albergues operate differently from private hostels. They generally do not accept advance reservations, and beds are allocated on arrival, subject to availability and the rules of the public network. Private albergues are usually the better option if you want to book a bed in advance.

Remember that the Camino gets particularly busy during the summer months and around major holiday periods. If you are travelling in July, August or September, booking private accommodation ahead can save you a lot of uncertainty at the end of a long day's walk.

Camino Portugués Hostel FAQs

Where does the last 100km of the Camino Portugués start?

Tui, in Galicia, is the traditional starting point for the final qualifying stretch of the Camino Portugués. The walk from Tui to Santiago is commonly described as approximately 120km, depending on the route and how distances are measured.

How many days does it take to walk from Tui to Santiago?

Most walkers take around five to seven days, depending on fitness, walking speed and how long they want to spend in each town. Six days is a popular option, with overnight stops around O Porriño, Redondela, Pontevedra, Caldas de Reis and Padrón.

Do I need to book hostels on the Camino Portugués?

You don't have to book every night, but it is a good idea during the busy season. Public albergues generally do not accept reservations, while private albergues and hostels usually do. Booking ahead gives you more control over where you stay, especially in popular towns such as Redondela, Pontevedra and Caldas de Reis.

What is the difference between an albergue and a hostel?

An albergue is usually a pilgrim-oriented hostel, often with shared dormitories and facilities designed for walkers. Some are public and operate under specific rules; others are privately run and accept advance bookings. A conventional backpacker hostel is more likely to have social common areas, private rooms and a wider range of facilities.

How much does a public albergue cost?

Public Xunta albergues on the Camino Portugués generally charge around €10 per night. They do not normally accept advance bookings, so beds are allocated on arrival, subject to availability.

Can I stay in a private hostel instead of a pilgrim albergue?

Yes. There are plenty of private hostels, guesthouses and hotels along the route. Some private albergues are exclusively for pilgrims, while others accept ordinary travellers too. Check the accommodation's booking conditions before reserving.

Is the Camino Portugués suitable for solo travellers?

Absolutely. The route is popular with solo walkers, and the shared accommodation makes it easy to meet other pilgrims. If meeting people is important to you, choose a private albergue with communal spaces or stay in the busier overnight towns.

Should I stay in Santiago or walk there from Padrón?

That depends on how you want to finish. Many pilgrims stay in Padrón and walk into Santiago the following day. Others prefer to stop somewhere between Padrón and the city, or continue all the way into Santiago. For recommendations once you arrive, see our separate guide to the best hostels in Santiago de Compostela.
