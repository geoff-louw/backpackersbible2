The Best Hostels in Granada

Hostelworld ratings and review counts checked: 13 November 2026. Facilities and contact details checked against hostel and official tourism sources where available.

Granada is one of Spain's great backpacker stops. The Alhambra dominates the skyline, the old Albaicín climbs the hillside in a maze of narrow lanes, and the city centre is full of tapas bars, student hangouts and late-night conversation. It is also a city where the hostel itself can be part of the experience: some places organise dinners and walking tours, while others are better for a quiet base between sightseeing days.

For an all-round social stay, HOST53 and El Granado are the strongest contenders. Black Swan Hostel is a good choice if you want organised group activities and a 24-hour reception. For a more distinctive setting, Oripandó is tucked into the Albaicín, while TOC Hostel puts you right by the Cathedral. Hostel Nut suits travellers who want coworking facilities, and Granada Old Town Hostel is a more straightforward, budget-conscious option.

This is a shortlist, not a directory of every place to sleep in the city. For more accommodation choices and the wider city guide, see our Granada backpacking guide and Andalusia hostel directory.

How we chose these hostels

We looked at current Hostelworld guest ratings and review volumes, then checked each property's location, facilities, social programme and official contact information. We have not copied across old ratings from other websites or invented missing contact details. Hostelworld scores are shown separately from our own Vibe Meter assessments.

About the Vibe Meters: these are BackpackersBible editorial assessments of the hostel's likely strengths, based on its published facilities and activities. They are not guest-review scores or independently measured statistics.

The Best Backpacker Hostels in Granada

Granada's hostel scene is more about shared dinners, tapas crawls, rooftop conversations and walking tours than enormous party-hostel complexes. These are the places that stand out for different kinds of backpacker.

HOST53
Best all-round hostel in Granada

  • Hostelworld Rating: 9.6 / 10 (3,023 reviews)
  • Location: 0.65 km from the city centre, according to Hostelworld
  • Booking.com Rating: 8.7 / 10 (710 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 9.6 / 10 (3,023 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.5 / 5 Stars (703 reviews)

What the rating tells you: HOST53's 9.6 score is supported by a large review sample. Hostelworld's category scores are particularly strong for staff (9.9), security (9.8) and location (9.8). The hostel describes itself as social without being a party hostel, which is an important distinction.

Best for: Backpackers who want a sociable, well-equipped base, plus digital nomads who value a dedicated workspace.

HOST53 combines accommodation with a café, gallery and workspaces in a restored building on Gran Vía de Colón. Its own published information lists shared and private rooms, a communal kitchen, lounge, cinema room, coworking space and regular social events. The programme can include free sangria, shared dinners, walking tours and hikes. It is a lively place to meet people, but the hostel says it keeps the sleeping areas quiet at night.

Not for you if: you are looking for a full-on party hostel with a bar or club running late into the night.

El Granado Hostel
Best for social travellers and solo backpackers

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.8 / 10 (559 reviews)
  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 9.6 / 10 (4,237 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.8 / 5 Stars (1,237 reviews)

What the rating tells you: El Granado has one of the largest review samples among Granada hostels and particularly strong Hostelworld scores for value (9.7), location (9.7) and atmosphere (9.5). Recent guest feedback highlights the organised activities and rooftop terraces.

Best for: Solo travellers and backpackers who want to meet people through shared activities without staying in a dedicated party hostel.

El Granado is a friendly, social hostel in the historic centre, with two rooftop terraces, a fully equipped kitchen, coworking space and common areas. Its published programme includes family dinners, flamenco, tapas tours, city walks, hikes and rooftop yoga. It offers dorms and a private double room, with air-conditioning and privacy curtains in the dorms.

Not for you if: you need a 24-hour staffed reception. The hostel lists reception hours as 09:00–21:00, with self check-in arrangements for late arrivals.

    Where to Stay in Granada

    Centro and the Cathedral area are the easiest base for first-time visitors. You can walk to the Cathedral, the main shopping streets, many tapas bars and the lower approaches to the Albaicín. HOST53, El Granado and TOC are all central choices, but they have noticeably different atmospheres.

    The Albaicín is Granada's atmospheric old Moorish quarter, with narrow cobbled lanes, whitewashed houses and viewpoints looking towards the Alhambra. Oripandó is a good fit if you want to stay in this historic neighbourhood. Be aware that it is steep in places and less convenient if you have heavy luggage.

    Realejo is the old Jewish quarter, with tapas bars, street art and a slightly more local feel. It is a useful area to explore in the evenings, even if you choose a hostel elsewhere in the centre.

    Sacromonte is famous for its hillside cave houses and flamenco venues. It is worth visiting, but the steep walk and more spread-out layout mean it is not necessarily the most practical base for a short stay.

    How Much Do Granada Hostels Cost?

    Hostelworld's current Granada listings show dorm beds starting at roughly €12–€20 per night at several of the hostels featured here. These are live starting prices, not a promise of what you will pay: dates, room type, demand and availability can change the rate considerably.

    Granada is a popular city break and student destination, so weekends, Easter, public holidays and major local festivals can push prices up. Book ahead if you are visiting during Semana Santa or the busy spring and summer periods.

    Check whether breakfast is included, whether towels are supplied, and whether reception will be open when you arrive. These small details can make a noticeable difference to the real cost and convenience of a cheap dorm bed.

    Granada Hostel FAQs

    What is the best hostel in Granada?

    HOST53 is our best all-round pick because it combines a central location, strong guest ratings, social activities, a café and coworking facilities. El Granado is an equally strong choice if organised social activities and rooftop terraces are your priority.

    What is the best hostel in Granada for solo travellers?

    El Granado and Black Swan Hostel Granada are particularly good options for solo travellers who want organised activities and opportunities to meet people. HOST53 is another strong option, with a social programme that the hostel describes as lively but not party-focused.

    Which Granada hostel is best for partying?

    Granada is not dominated by huge party hostels in the way some European cities are. Black Swan lists group activities and nights out, while El Granado runs dinners, tapas tours and flamenco activities. HOST53 also runs regular social events, but explicitly describes itself as a social hostel rather than a party hostel.

    What is the best area to stay in Granada?

    For a first visit, the city centre around the Cathedral is the most practical base. Choose the Albaicín if atmosphere and historic surroundings matter more than flat streets and easy luggage access.

    Are Granada hostels suitable for solo female travellers?

    Several hostels offer female-only dorms, including Oripandó and Black Swan; El Granado also lists a female-only dorm. Check the room type when booking, use the lockers provided, and read recent reviews before choosing a property.

    Do Granada hostels have kitchens?

    Many do. HOST53, El Granado, Black Swan, TOC, Oripandó, Hostel Nut and Granada Old Town Hostel all list shared kitchen facilities. Check each property's current rules and opening arrangements before relying on a kitchen for every meal.

    How much is a hostel in Granada?

    Current starting prices on Hostelworld are roughly €12–€20 for dorm beds at the hostels featured here, but actual prices depend on your dates and room choice. Summer weekends and festival dates can be more expensive.

    Is the Albaicín a good place to stay?

    Yes, if you want historic atmosphere, small plazas and views towards the Alhambra. It is hilly, with cobbled lanes, so it is less convenient if you have heavy bags or prefer easy, level walks. Oripandó is one option in the neighbourhood.

    When should I book a hostel in Granada?

    Book early for Semana Santa, spring weekends and major festival periods. For quieter dates you may find availability closer to arrival, but compare the cancellation conditions and check-in hours before you commit.
