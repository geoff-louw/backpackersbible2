The Best Hostels in Santiago de Compostela

Ratings and hostel information checked: 13 November 2026.

Santiago de Compostela is the grand finale of the Camino de Santiago, but you don't have to be walking a pilgrimage to enjoy this beautiful Galician city. Its medieval streets, lively tapas bars, historic squares and international atmosphere make it an interesting stop for independent travellers exploring northern Spain.

The hostel scene is a little different from Barcelona or Madrid. Many places cater primarily to pilgrims arriving at the end of their Camino, rather than conventional backpackers looking for a party. That said, there are some excellent places to stay, including sociable hostels with gardens and communal kitchens, modern backpacker accommodation and a few characterful old buildings.

If you're looking to meet other travellers, Meiga Backpackers is our pick for social atmosphere. Blanco Albergue is a particularly appealing choice for comfort and cleanliness, while Hostal Seminario Menor offers one of the most unusual hostel experiences in Galicia. For a central location, it's hard to beat The Last Stamp.

This guide covers the best hostels in Santiago for socialising, solo travellers, groups, comfort and location, with ratings, neighbourhood advice, typical prices and direct contact details. For accommodation along the Camino routes, see our Camino de Santiago hostel directory.

How we chose these hostels

We compared Santiago's hostels using guest ratings on Hostelworld, Booking.com and Google, as well as location, social atmosphere, facilities, cleanliness, value for money and direct booking options. We prioritised places that offer something worthwhile to independent travellers, rather than simply listing every pilgrim albergue in the city.

Santiago has a relatively small hostel market, and some properties have far fewer reviews than the big-city hostels featured in our other guides. We take this into account when comparing ratings. Prices and availability change throughout the year, so confirm the latest details directly with the hostel before booking.

Best Social Hostels in Santiago de Compostela

Santiago isn't particularly famous for its party hostels, but there are a few good places to meet other travellers. If you've just finished a long Camino, or are simply passing through Galicia, these are the hostels where you're most likely to find people to share a meal, a drink or a few stories with.

Meiga Backpackers Hostel
Best social hostel in Santiago

  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 8.2 / 10 (1,425 reviews)
  • Booking.com Rating: 7.4 / 10 (607 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.1 / 5 Stars (441 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Meiga's Hostelworld reviews highlight its friendly staff, welcoming atmosphere and garden, with staff scoring particularly well at 9.0. Its atmosphere score of 8.1 suggests a genuinely sociable hostel rather than a full-on party establishment. Some guests mention cramped bathrooms and the location being a little further from the old town, so it's not quite perfect, but it's one of Santiago's better options for meeting other travellers.

Best for: Solo backpackers and independent travellers who want a friendly, sociable place with communal spaces and organised activities.

Meiga is one of the more genuinely backpacker-oriented hostels in Santiago. Its facilities include a communal kitchen, spacious lounge, garden with hammocks, barbecue area, games, guitars and a book exchange. The hostel also organises concerts, communal dinners and other events, making it easier to meet people than in many of the city's more traditional pilgrim albergues.

It's around a 10–15 minute walk from the cathedral, in the direction of the San Pedro neighbourhood. The garden is a particularly nice bonus during warmer weather, and there's bicycle storage for anyone travelling on two wheels.

Not for you if: You want to be right in the middle of the old town or expect a proper party hostel. Meiga is sociable, but its atmosphere is more relaxed than rowdy.

  • Nearest bus: Rúa Pastoriza

Blanco Albergue
Best hostel for comfort and cleanliness

  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 10 / 10 (347 reviews)
  • Booking.com Rating: 8.5 / 10 (860 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.5 / 5 Stars (841 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Blanco's perfect Hostelworld score is particularly impressive for a small hostel, with guests awarding top marks for cleanliness, security, staff and location. The relatively modest number of reviews means the score should be treated with a little caution, but the feedback consistently praises the comfortable beds, privacy curtains, peaceful atmosphere and exceptionally clean facilities.

Best for: Travellers who appreciate a clean, comfortable bed, a little privacy and a peaceful place to recover after a long journey.

Blanco Albergue is a small, modern hostel in a particularly convenient location, just a few minutes' walk from the cathedral and historic centre. Its dormitories have individual reading lights, power sockets, privacy curtains and personal lockers, while separate male and female showers are a welcome bonus.

The communal facilities include a lounge, television, computer, small kitchen area and laundry. It's a particularly good option if you've spent several weeks sharing basic pilgrim accommodation and fancy somewhere a little more comfortable for your final nights in Santiago.

Not for you if: You're looking for a big social scene, organised pub crawls or a party atmosphere. Blanco is more about comfort and a good night's sleep.

  • Nearest bus: Central Santiago bus routes

  • Website: Official Blanco Albergue website / direct bookings

Hostal Seminario Menor
Best hostel for character and atmosphere

  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 8.3 / 10 (380 reviews)
  • Booking.com Rating: 8.4 / 10 (7,798 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.2 / 5 Stars (1,595 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Seminario Menor's strong Hostelworld score is supported by particularly good feedback for cleanliness, staff and facilities. Its location is a little further from the cathedral than some of the smaller central hostels, but the building itself is a major part of the attraction. This is a good choice if you'd rather stay somewhere with a bit of history than in a conventional modern hostel.

Best for: Travellers who enjoy unusual accommodation, historic buildings and the atmosphere of a place filled with people completing the Camino.

The Albergue Seminario Menor occupies a former seminary, a huge institutional building with an unmistakable character. It has around 177 individual beds, social rooms with televisions, laundry facilities, vending machines, bicycle parking and lockers.

One of its biggest attractions is its position overlooking the city from the Belvís side of Santiago. It's roughly a ten-minute walk from the cathedral, and the surrounding park provides some lovely views of the old town.

This is primarily a pilgrim hostel, so the atmosphere is a little different from a conventional backpacker establishment. Nevertheless, its size and communal facilities mean there's usually no shortage of fellow travellers to chat to.

Not for you if: You want boutique-hostel comforts, a lively bar or a modern party atmosphere. This is a large, traditional pilgrim accommodation building, and it has its own rules and opening hours.

The Last Stamp
Best hostel for location

  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 8.6 / 10 (50 reviews)
  • Booking.com Rating: 8 / 10 (1,937 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 3.9 / 5 Stars (437 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: The Last Stamp's biggest selling point is its exceptional location, with Hostelworld guests giving it a perfect 10 for location. Staff also score particularly well. Its relatively small review sample means the overall rating is less established than some of the city's larger hostels, but the central position is difficult to beat.

Best for: Backpackers who want to stay right in the historic centre, close to the cathedral, restaurants, bars and Santiago's main attractions.

The Last Stamp occupies a renovated stone building in the heart of Santiago's old town, just a few minutes from the cathedral. It's a particularly convenient choice if you want to explore the city on foot without having to worry about transport or walking back to your accommodation at night.

The hostel offers dormitory beds and private rooms, with individual lighting, power sockets and lockers. There's also a shared kitchen, dining space and terrace. Its old-town location makes it an especially good choice for anyone who wants to spend their time exploring Santiago's medieval streets, rather than hanging around in the hostel.

Not for you if: You prefer a quiet neighbourhood away from tourist traffic. The central location is wonderful for sightseeing, but can also mean more noise from the surrounding streets.

Loop INN Hostel Santiago
Best modern all-rounder

  • Hostelworld.com Rating: 8.6 / 10 (50 reviews)
  • Booking.com Rating: 8 / 10 (1,937 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.1 / 5 Stars (1,951 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Loop INN has a strong Hostelworld score and a good reputation as a practical, comfortable base in the city. Its central location and range of facilities make it a reliable option for travellers who want something more conventional and predictable than a basic pilgrim albergue.

Best for: Backpackers who want modern facilities, a convenient location and a comfortable place to relax after exploring Santiago.

Loop INN is a modern hostel in the historic centre, close to the Convent of Santa Clara and within walking distance of the cathedral. It offers a mixture of shared and private accommodation, with facilities including a television room, games, laundry, bicycle facilities and vending machines.

It's a sensible all-round choice, particularly if you're staying for more than one night and want a comfortable base from which to explore the city. The atmosphere is more relaxed than that of a dedicated party hostel, but its shared facilities provide opportunities to meet other travellers.

Not for you if: You're looking for a particularly quirky hostel or a big organised social programme. Loop INN is more about convenient, comfortable accommodation than backpacker entertainment.

Dream in Santiago
Best hostel for groups

  • Booking.com Rating: 8.9 / 10 (3,295 reviews)
  • Google Places Rating: 4.6 / 5 Stars (452 reviews)

What the ratings tell you: Dream in Santiago's excellent Booking.com score and positive Google feedback reflect its modern facilities, friendly staff and comfortable accommodation. It has a particularly good reputation with guests travelling in groups, and its private dormitory arrangements make it more flexible than many traditional pilgrim hostels.

Best for: Groups of friends, cycling groups and backpackers who want to book an entire dormitory rather than share with strangers.

Dream in Santiago is a modern hostel at the entrance to the city, near the Camino Francés approach. It has 68 beds, with individual privacy curtains, reading lights, power sockets and USB connections. The big selling point is its ability to accommodate groups in private dormitories of different sizes, sleeping up to 30 people.

There's a garden, terrace, communal kitchen and dining area, along with bicycle parking and free luggage storage for guests. The accommodation has a friendly international atmosphere, and the outdoor space is a welcome bonus during the warmer months.

The location is less central than the other hostels in this guide, but it's convenient for people arriving along the Camino Francés and for anyone who doesn't mind taking a bus or walking into the historic centre.

Not for you if: You want to stay right beside the cathedral or walk straight out into Santiago's bars and restaurants. This is a more practical base on the outskirts of the city.

Where to Stay in Santiago de Compostela

Historic centre: The best area if you want to be close to the cathedral, restaurants, tapas bars and the city's main attractions. The Last Stamp is particularly well positioned here. You'll be able to explore most of the old town on foot, although accommodation can be a little more expensive.

San Pedro and Basquiños: A good alternative to the busy historic centre, with a more residential neighbourhood atmosphere. It's still within walking distance of the cathedral, and Meiga Backpackers is a good choice if you want a sociable hostel with outdoor space.

Belvís: A pleasant area just outside the old town, with green spaces and views across Santiago. Hostal Seminario Menor is the obvious choice here, particularly if you like historic buildings and don't mind a short walk into the centre.

Galeras: A convenient location on the western side of the old town, close to the cathedral and the main historic attractions. Blanco Albergue is a particularly good choice if you want a quieter place to stay without being far from the action.

San Lázaro: On the eastern approach to the city, this is a practical option for groups and travellers arriving along the Camino Francés. Dream in Santiago is located here, although you'll need to walk or take public transport to reach the historic centre.

How Much Do Hostels in Santiago Cost?

Santiago is generally cheaper than Spain's major backpacker cities, although prices vary considerably according to the season and the number of pilgrims arriving in town.

As a rough guide, expect to pay around €13–€25 per night for a dorm bed in a budget hostel or pilgrim albergue. More modern hostels and central accommodation can cost around €20–€35, while private rooms are generally more expensive.

The busiest period is usually from May to September, with particularly strong demand during July and August. The Feast of St James on 25 July is one of the city's biggest annual celebrations, and accommodation can become difficult to find around this date.

If you're walking the Camino, don't assume that the cheapest pilgrim accommodation will necessarily be available when you arrive. Some albergues operate on a first-come, first-served basis, while others accept advance reservations. Check the rules before setting off.

For a comfortable backpacker budget, allow around €45–€70 per day for a hostel dorm, food, drinks and sightseeing, depending on how much you spend eating out.

Santiago de Compostela Hostel FAQs

What is the best hostel in Santiago de Compostela?

Meiga Backpackers is our pick for social atmosphere and meeting other travellers. Blanco Albergue is a particularly good option for comfort and cleanliness, while The Last Stamp is hard to beat for its central location.

What is the best hostel in Santiago for solo travellers?

Meiga Backpackers is a good choice for solo travellers because of its communal kitchen, garden, shared lounge and organised activities. If you're more interested in a comfortable, peaceful stay than socialising, Blanco Albergue is another excellent option.

Are there party hostels in Santiago de Compostela?

What is the best area to stay in Santiago?

How much does a hostel cost in Santiago de Compostela?

Should I book a hostel in Santiago in advance?

Yes, particularly during July and August, around the Feast of St James on 25 July, and during busy Camino periods. Santiago receives large numbers of pilgrims, and the better hostels can fill up quickly. Booking ahead is particularly sensible if you want a specific hostel or private room.

Can you stay in Santiago after finishing the Camino?

Absolutely. There's no need to leave as soon as you've collected your Compostela. Santiago is a beautiful city with plenty to see and do, and staying an extra night or two gives you time to explore the old town, enjoy some Galician food and celebrate reaching the end of your journey.
