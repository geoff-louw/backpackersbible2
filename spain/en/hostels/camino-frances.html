A quick note about Spanish accommodation: a hostel, hostal and albergue are not necessarily the same thing. Hostels are the familiar backpacker option, while a hostal is usually a cheap private guesthouse. Albergue can mean a youth hostel, municipal hostel, mountain refuge or Camino pilgrim hostel, each with different rules. Regional languages can add to the confusion too — for example, Catalonia uses alberg and the Basque Country uses aterpetxe. There are also networks such as REAJ, Hostelling International and Catalonia's Xanascat.

ALBERGUE MUNICIPAL DE PEREGRINOS DE SARRIA

STREET ADDRESS: Rúa Maior, 79, 27600 Sarria, Lugo

This is the public pilgrims’ albergue in Sarria, run by the Xunta de Galicia in an old stone house on the town’s main street, Rúa Maior. It is for pilgrims only, so you need a pilgrim credential (credencial). Beds cannot be reserved: it is first come, first served, and with 40 places in a single dormitory it can fill early when walkers finishing a stage arrive at the same time as those starting their Camino here. It is open all year, from 13:00 until it closes at 22:00, though hours can change, so check locally. There is a kitchen (without utensils) with a microwave, a washing machine and dryer, and disposable sheets, and the building is accessible to guests with reduced mobility.
