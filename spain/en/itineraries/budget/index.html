Budget Itineraries

Backpacking Spain on a Budget

Spain is one of the few big countries where a budget traveller does not need a car. The high-speed rail network, the coaches and the hostels are so good that most backpackers see the country on public transport, and the itineraries below are built around that. There are three here: the cheapest way to see the country in two weeks, the same two weeks with the rough edges removed, and a ten-day road trip for four friends sharing a hire car.

All costs are in euros per person for 2026 and were checked in October 2026. Accommodation is in dorm beds unless stated otherwise. Train fares move with demand, so the prices here are the sort you can expect if you book a few weeks ahead; last-minute tickets can cost two or three times as much. Flights to and from Spain are not included.

A heads-up on Granada: a series of earthquakes in August 2026 closed parts of the Alhambra and several other monuments in the city. The Alhambra's Alcazaba reopened on 23 September 2026 on an adapted route, but visitor routes can change, and some churches and the cathedral were closed for checks. Confirm opening on the official sites before you book.

Looking for something else? These pages are part of our Spain itineraries collection, with more routes by theme, region and trip length on the main itineraries page.

2 WEEKS: The Shoestring Tour

The cheapest itinerary on the site, and a proper one: six cities in fourteen days, from Barcelona to Madrid via Valencia, Granada, Seville and Córdoba, with no flights inside Spain and no hire car. Everything moves by bus and train, you sleep in dorms with kitchens, breakfast comes from a supermarket and lunch is a menú del día. It works out at about €52 a day all in, and it still includes the Alhambra. The route is one-way, so fly into Barcelona and out of Madrid (or the reverse) — an open-jaw ticket usually costs little more than a return.

Day 1: Arrive in Barcelona
Take the metro from the airport (line L9 Sud, with the airport supplement) or the Aerobús if your hostel is near Plaça de Catalunya. Drop your bags, walk down to the sea at Barceloneta and eat early; Spanish dinners start late and the cheapest menus finish by 9pm.
Suggested accommodation: Sant Jordi Hostel Gràcia, Barcelona
Daily distance: about 15km from the airport.

Day 2: Barcelona on foot
Join a tip-based walking tour of the Gothic Quarter, then wander El Born, Santa Maria del Mar and the Ciutadella park. Finish at the Bunkers del Carmel, a free hilltop viewpoint with the best sunset in the city — bring supermarket beers and ignore the street sellers.
Suggested accommodation: Sant Jordi Hostel Gràcia, Barcelona
Daily distance: about 10km on foot.

Day 3: Gràcia, Montjuïc and the Gaudí exteriors
Spend the morning in Gràcia's squares, then see the Sagrada Família from outside (free) and the open surroundings of Park Güell. In the afternoon take the escalators up Montjuïc for the views and, in the evening, the free Magic Fountain show (check the current season's schedule). If you want to go inside the Sagrada Família it costs €26 for basic entry (€24 for under-30s and students) and is timed and online-only, but this itinerary skips it to protect the budget.
Suggested accommodation: Sant Jordi Hostel Gràcia, Barcelona
Daily distance: about 10km on foot, plus metro.

Day 4: Barcelona to Valencia
A morning train or bus down the coast, 3 to 4.5 hours depending on service. Train fares from around €25 if booked early; coach fares from around €15–25 on FlixBus or Alsa. Arrive in time for a walk through the Turia Gardens, the old river bed turned into a 9km park that runs through the city.
Suggested accommodation: Red Nest Hostel, Valencia
Daily distance: 350km by rail or coach.

Day 5: Valencia
Breakfast at the Mercado Central, then the street art of the Carmen quarter and the outside of the City of Arts and Sciences (free to look at). Take a city bus out to the Albufera lagoon for sunset and eat paella in its home territory; the proper version uses rabbit and chicken, not seafood.
Suggested accommodation: Red Nest Hostel, Valencia
Daily distance: about 25km including the Albufera.

Day 6: Valencia to Granada
The long day of the trip: Alsa runs a direct coach of roughly 7.5 to 8 hours, about €35–40 if booked ahead. Take food and a charged phone. Granada rewards you on arrival — in many of its bars a free tapa still arrives with every drink, so dinner can cost the price of two beers.
Suggested accommodation: Granada Inn Backpackers, Granada
Daily distance: about 500km by coach.

Day 7: The Alhambra
Book the General ticket with Nasrid Palaces slot on the official site as soon as your dates are fixed; it costs about €22 online and the palace slots sell out weeks ahead in season. Arrive for your slot, then explore the Generalife gardens and the Alcazaba, which reopened on 23 September 2026 after August's earthquakes on an adapted route. Watch the sun go down from the Mirador de San Nicolás in the Albaicín.
Suggested accommodation: Granada Inn Backpackers, Granada
Daily distance: about 8km on foot.

Day 8: Granada for free
Walk the Darro river path from Paseo de los Tristes, climb through the Sacromonte neighbourhood and eat free tapas in the Realejo. Several Granada monuments, including the cathedral, were closed for structural checks after the August earthquakes, so confirm opening before you plan to go in.
Suggested accommodation: Granada Inn Backpackers, Granada
Daily distance: about 8km on foot.

Day 9: Granada to Seville
A morning Alsa coach across Andalusia, about 3 hours and €18–25. Arrive in time for the Santa Cruz quarter at dusk, when the orange-scented alleys are at their best.
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Hostel Sevilla, Seville
Daily distance: 250km by coach.

Day 10: Seville
Plaza de España and the María Luisa Park in the morning, then cross the river to Triana for the market and a long lunch. In the evening, La Carbonería in Santa Cruz hosts informal flamenco in a free-entry bar; arrive early for a seat.
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Hostel Sevilla, Seville
Daily distance: about 10km on foot.

Day 11: Seville to Córdoba
A short hop on the train or coach, from 45 minutes to 2 hours and €10–25 depending on service. Spend the afternoon at the Mezquita-Catedral (about €15, with a free early-morning slot on most days — check the current hours), the Roman Bridge and the white lanes of the Judería.
Suggested accommodation: Córdoba hostels
Daily distance: 140km by rail.

Day 12: Córdoba to Madrid
A morning high-speed train to the capital in about two hours. Book Ouigo or Iryo in advance for €15–35; the same ticket turned up on the day can cost three times as much. Settle in and walk down to the Plaza Mayor for a bocadillo de calamares, the city's cheap signature sandwich.
Suggested accommodation: Cat's Hostel, Madrid
Daily distance: 400km by rail.

Day 13: Madrid
Retiro Park in the morning, then the Prado and Reina Sofía on their free evening slots (check current hours, and arrive early for the queue). Watch sunset at the Templo de Debod and eat in Lavapiés. If it's a Sunday, spend the morning at El Rastro flea market.
Suggested accommodation: Cat's Hostel, Madrid
Daily distance: about 10km on foot.

Day 14: Madrid and home
Spend the morning in the Malasaña and La Latina neighbourhoods, then take the metro or Cercanías to the airport. Aim to be there at least two hours before an international flight.
Daily distance: about 15km to the airport.

Estimated Cost Breakdown (Per Person, 2026)

Based on dorm beds throughout and a solo traveller, with prices checked in October 2026. Flights to and from Spain are not included.

Accommodation (13 nights in dorms, about €25 a night on average, higher in Barcelona and Madrid, lower in Granada):
€325

Intercity transport (Barcelona–Valencia €20, Valencia–Granada €38, Granada–Seville €20, Seville–Córdoba €13, Córdoba–Madrid €25):
€116

Local transport and airport transfers:
€25

Food and drink (about €16/day: supermarket breakfast, menú del día lunch, free tapas in Granada):
€224

Activities (Alhambra €22, Mezquita-Catedral €15, one paid museum €8; everything else is free):
€45

Total estimated trip cost: ~€735 per person (approximately €52/day, which makes this the cheapest itinerary on the site and still includes the Alhambra.)

Cheaper still: Hostel work exchange can cut the accommodation bill to almost nothing (typically a few hours’ work a day for a bed, with a minimum stay). Dropping Barcelona and starting in Madrid saves about €150. And in Granada, a drink with a free tapa is a legitimate dinner.

Rail fares: Spanish rail pricing follows demand. On Madrid–Barcelona, average fares in late 2025 were about €61 on Ouigo, €77 on Iryo and €96 on Renfe's AVE, with advance fares from around €19. Book as early as you can, and note that most Ouigo fares cannot be changed or refunded.

Pickpockets: Barcelona's metro, the Ramblas and the beach are the hotspots. Keep your phone in a front pocket or zipped bag, and never leave it on a café table.

2 WEEKS: The Flashpacker's Spain

The same country with the friction removed. You take high-speed trains rather than coaches, sleep in design hostels and boutique guesthouses in private rooms, eat dinner sitting down and book the experiences that are worth it: the Sagrada Família, a flamenco show, a cooking class, the Arab baths in Granada. It runs from Barcelona to Málaga via Madrid, Seville and Granada, and works out at roughly €113 a day. Fly into Barcelona and out of Málaga.

Read More

Day 1: Arrive in Barcelona
Take a taxi or the Aerobús to your hostel, drop your bags and walk through Gràcia, the neighbourhood where locals actually live. Book an early dinner and go to bed; tomorrow is a big day.
Suggested accommodation: Generator Barcelona (private room)
Daily distance: about 15km from the airport.

Day 2: Gaudí day
Book timed tickets online well ahead. The Sagrada Família costs €26 for basic entry including the audio guide (€24 for under-30s and students, €36 with a tower), and sells out weeks ahead in this centenary year. Park Güell's monumental zone costs about €10–12 and also needs a timed ticket. Have a menú del día in Gràcia in between.
Suggested accommodation: Generator Barcelona (private room)
Daily distance: about 8km on foot, plus metro.

Day 3: Gothic Quarter and the Picasso Museum
Walk the Gothic Quarter in the morning, then the Picasso Museum in El Born (book ahead). In the evening take a tapas and vermouth tour, about €55, which is usually the best money you'll spend in the city.
Suggested accommodation: Generator Barcelona (private room)
Daily distance: about 10km on foot.

Day 4: Montserrat
A day trip to the serrated mountain monastery. The R5 train plus the Cremallera rack railway costs about €30 return from Barcelona, and the monastery and its walking trails reward the early start. Back in Barcelona for dinner.
Suggested accommodation: Generator Barcelona (private room)
Daily distance: about 120km return by rail.

Day 5: Barcelona to Madrid
A high-speed train to the capital in 2.5 to 3 hours. Choose a flexible fare (€55–80 on Iryo or AVE) if your plans might move; Ouigo is cheaper but non-changeable. Evening drinks and tapas in La Latina.
Suggested accommodation: The Hat Madrid (private room)
Daily distance: 620km by rail.

Day 6: Madrid's art triangle
The Prado (€15) and the Reina Sofía (€12, home of Picasso's Guernica) are close enough to do on the same day. Finish with a drink on the Círculo de Bellas Artes rooftop terrace (about €5 to go up) for the best free-ish skyline in the city.
Suggested accommodation: The Hat Madrid (private room)
Daily distance: about 9km on foot.

Day 7: Toledo day trip
Toledo's walled old town is 35 to 60 minutes from Madrid by Alsa coach or Avant train, around €12 return. Cathedral, synagogues, swords and marzipan. Back in Madrid for the evening.
Suggested accommodation: The Hat Madrid (private room)
Daily distance: about 140km return.

Day 8: Madrid to Seville
A high-speed train south in about 2.5 hours, €35–75 depending on how early you book. Check in, then walk through Santa Cruz at dusk.
Suggested accommodation: Samay Hostel Sevilla (private room)
Daily distance: 530km by rail.

Day 9: Seville's Alcázar and a flamenco night
Book the Real Alcázar ahead (around €19) and arrive first thing, before the tour groups. Climb the Giralda in the afternoon and finish with an evening flamenco show in a Triana tablao (about €30–40).
Suggested accommodation: Samay Hostel Sevilla (private room)
Daily distance: about 8km on foot.

Day 10: Cooking class and Triana
A half-day Andalusian cooking class (about €55–65) that starts with a market tour and ends with lunch. Spend the afternoon in Triana's ceramic workshops, then take it easy at the hostel's rooftop.
Suggested accommodation: Samay Hostel Sevilla (private room)
Daily distance: about 6km on foot.

Day 11: Seville to Granada
A morning coach across the Andalusian plain, about 3 hours and €20–25. Eat tapas in the Realejo as soon as you've dropped your bags.
Suggested accommodation: Makuto Guesthouse (private room)
Daily distance: 250km by coach.

Day 12: The Alhambra and the Arab baths
Book the Alhambra's General ticket with Nasrid Palaces slot (about €22 online) as early as you can. The Alcazaba reopened on 23 September 2026 on an adapted route after August's earthquakes. In the evening, soak in the candlelit Arab baths (about €35–50), a Granada institution.
Suggested accommodation: Makuto Guesthouse (private room)
Daily distance: about 8km on foot.

Day 13: Granada to Málaga
A short coach to the coast, about 1.5 hours and €12. Visit the Picasso Museum (about €12) and the Alcazaba, then swim and eat fish at the beach — espetos (sardines grilled on skewers over a fire on the sand) are the local speciality.
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Palace (private room)
Daily distance: 130km by coach.

Day 14: Málaga and home
Breakfast at the Atarazanas market, then the short Cercanías train to the airport.
Daily distance: about 10km to the airport.

Estimated Cost Breakdown (Per Person, 2026)

Based on two people sharing private rooms (priced per person), with prices checked in October 2026. Flights to and from Spain are not included.

Accommodation (13 nights in private rooms, about €45 per person on average):
€590

Intercity transport (Barcelona–Madrid €55, Madrid–Seville €50, Seville–Granada €22, Granada–Málaga €13):
€140

Day trips (Montserrat €30, Toledo €12) and local transport:
€75

Food and drink (about €38/day, with sit-down dinners and a tapas tour):
€532

Activities (Sagrada Família €26, Park Güell €11, Prado €15, Reina Sofía €12, Alhambra €22, Alcázar €19, flamenco €35, cooking class €60, Arab baths €40, Picasso Museum €12):
€250

Total estimated trip cost: ~€1,590 per person (approximately €113/day. Solo travellers should add roughly 40–50% to the room costs.)

Sagrada Família: Tickets are online-only and timed, and in 2026 they have been selling out as much as two months ahead because of Gaudí's centenary. Book as soon as your dates are fixed, and avoid unofficial resellers who add up to €15.

Alhambra: The Nasrid Palaces slot is the one that sells out. If the General ticket is gone, the Gardens ticket (about €13) is usually still available, but it does not include the palaces.

10 DAYS: Four Friends and a Fiat

Rail and coach are the cheapest way to travel Spain alone, but four people sharing one hire car changes the sums. This Andalusia loop starts and ends in Málaga and uses the car where it earns its keep — the Caminito del Rey, the white villages of Ronda and Setenil, and the Alpujarras valleys in the Sierra Nevada, none of which a bus timetable serves well — then leaves it in a garage in the big cities. It works out at about €64 a day each, and you can sleep four to a room to keep the accommodation low.

Read More

Day 1: Arrive in Málaga
Spend the afternoon on the beach and the evening in the old town, eating espetos at the beach bars. You do not need the car yet, and Málaga's centre is not a place to drive.
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Palace, Málaga
Daily distance: no driving.

Day 2: Málaga to Ronda via the Caminito del Rey
Collect the car in the morning. Drive to El Chorro for the Caminito del Rey, a walkway pinned to a gorge wall 100 metres above the river. It costs about €10–12, is timed and must be booked weeks ahead; arrive 30 minutes early and bring ID. Continue to Ronda in the late afternoon and watch the sun set over the Puente Nuevo.
Suggested accommodation: A four-bed room in a hostal in Ronda's old town (about €80–100 a night, split four ways)
Daily distance: about 150km.

Day 3: Ronda, Setenil and Seville
Walk the gorge viewpoints in the morning, then drive 15 minutes to Setenil de las Bodegas, where whole streets run beneath overhanging rock. Then continue to Seville, and leave the car in a garage on the edge of the old town.
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Hostel Sevilla, Seville (four-bed room)
Daily distance: about 150km.

Day 4: Seville
Leave the car parked. Do Plaza de España and the María Luisa Park in the morning, the Real Alcázar (around €19, book ahead) in the afternoon, and flamenco at La Carbonería in the evening.
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Hostel Sevilla, Seville (four-bed room)
Daily distance: no driving.

Day 5: Seville to Córdoba
An easy drive up the Guadalquivir valley. Spend the afternoon in the Mezquita-Catedral (about €15), the Roman Bridge and the Judería, and leave the car in a car park outside the old town.
Suggested accommodation: Córdoba hostels (four-bed room or apartment)
Daily distance: about 140km.

Day 6: Córdoba to Granada
Take the A-92 east through olive country; the hills are covered in trees for much of the way. Granada has a camera-enforced low-emission zone in the centre, so ask your accommodation to register the car's plate and park outside the zone.
Suggested accommodation: Granada Inn Backpackers, Granada (four-bed room)
Daily distance: about 165km.

Day 7: The Alhambra
Book the General ticket with Nasrid Palaces slot (about €22 online) weeks ahead. The Alcazaba reopened on 23 September 2026 on an adapted route after August's earthquakes. Evening free tapas in the Realejo.
Suggested accommodation: Granada Inn Backpackers, Granada (four-bed room)
Daily distance: no driving.

Day 8: Granada to the Alpujarras
Drive up through Lanjarón (spring water) to the white villages of the Poqueira gorge — Capileira, Bubión and Pampaneira — and walk between them. The roads are narrow and steep; take your time.
Suggested accommodation: A four-bed room or village apartment in Capileira or Pampaneira (about €70–90 a night, split four ways)
Daily distance: about 95km.

Day 9: The Alpujarras to Nerja
Drive down to the coast via Órgiva and Motril, then along the coast road to Frigiliana, a whitewashed hill village above Nerja, for lunch. Swim in one of the coves below the Nerja cliffs in the afternoon.
Suggested accommodation: A four-bed room or apartment in Nerja (about €80–100 a night, split four ways)
Daily distance: about 150km.

Day 10: Nerja to Málaga
A short, easy drive back along the coast. Return the car at the airport, then fly out.
Daily distance: about 55km.

Estimated Cost Breakdown (Per Person, 2026)

Based on four people sharing the car, rooms and fuel, with prices checked in October 2026. Flights to and from Spain are not included.

Car hire (9 days, compact car, full insurance, Málaga return; about €360 for the car, split four ways):
€90

Fuel (about 950km including detours, at roughly €1.50–1.60 a litre; about €90 for the car):
€22

Parking (city garages in Seville, Córdoba and Granada; about €85 for the car):
€21

Accommodation (9 nights in four-bed rooms or apartments, about €22.50 per person per night):
€203

Food and drink (about €20/day, self-catering for breakfast and one cooked dinner):
€200

Activities (Caminito del Rey €11, Alhambra €22, Alcázar €19, Mezquita-Catedral €15, miscellaneous €18):
€85

Airport transfers and miscellaneous:
€20

Total estimated trip cost: ~€641 per person (approximately €64/day, which is higher than the Shoestring Tour per day but buys you villages and valleys the buses do not reach.)

Under-25 drivers: Many hire companies add a young-driver surcharge, typically in the region of €10–25 a day, and a few will not rent to under-21s at all. Put a driver aged 25 or over on the contract, and check the excess and fuel policy before you pay.

Drinking and driving: Spain's blood-alcohol limit is 0.5g/l (0.3g/l for drivers with less than two years' licence), and roadside testing is common. Nominate a driver each day.

Tolls: This loop uses toll-free autovías throughout. If you are tempted to add Cádiz or the Costa del Sol by motorway, check whether the road is a toll road first.
