Backpacking Spain Itineraries

Spain is one of the easiest countries in Europe to backpack: the trains are fast, the coaches are cheap and the hostels are good. The hard part is choosing a route. Here are twelve, from a five-day city stay to a three-month grand tour, with interactive maps, daily distances, suggested places to sleep and a cost breakdown for each.

All costs are in euros per person for 2026, checked in October 2026, and based on dorm beds and a solo traveller unless stated. Flights to and from Spain are not included. Train fares move with demand, so the prices here are what to expect if you book a few weeks ahead; last-minute tickets can cost two or three times as much. If you have two weeks and want the cheapest way to see the country, start with the Shoestring Tour; for private rooms and a few treats, try the Flashpacker's Spain.

A heads-up on Granada: a series of earthquakes in August 2026 closed parts of the Alhambra. The Alcazaba reopened on 23 September 2026 on an adapted route, but visitor routes can change, and the cathedral and some churches were closed for checks. Confirm opening on the official sites before you book.

5 DAYS: Barcelona City Stay

One base, no luggage-dragging, and the best city beach in Europe. Barcelona rewards a five-day stay: Gaudí, the medieval Gothic Quarter, Picasso, a mountain monastery and long evenings in Gràcia's squares, all within a metro ride of one hostel. This is a city itinerary, so the daily distances are walking and metro, and it works out at about €68 a day. 2026 is the centenary of Gaudí's death and the city is busier than ever, so book the big tickets and your bed early.

Day 1: Arrive in Barcelona
Take the metro from the airport (line L9 Sud). The airport needs its own ticket, €5.90 one way in 2026, because the T-casual 10-trip card is not valid to the airport. Check in, walk down through the Gràcia squares and eat early; the cheapest menus stop at about 9pm.
Suggested accommodation: Sant Jordi Hostel Gràcia, Barcelona
Daily distance: about 15km from the airport.

Day 2: The Gothic Quarter, El Born and the beach
Join a tip-based walking tour of the Gothic Quarter, then cross into El Born for Santa Maria del Mar and the Picasso Museum (book ahead; prices and free slots change, so check the museum's site). Walk on to Barceloneta for a swim, and finish at the Bunkers del Carmel, a free hilltop viewpoint with the best sunset in the city.
Suggested accommodation: Sant Jordi Hostel Gràcia, Barcelona
Daily distance: about 12km on foot.

Day 3: Gaudí
Book timed tickets online well in advance. The Sagrada Família costs €26 for basic entry including the audio guide (€24 for under-30s and students, €36 with a tower), and sells out weeks ahead in the centenary year. Park Güell's monumental zone costs €18 after a steep rise in 2026, and the rest of the park is free. Casa Batlló (from €29) and La Pedrera (from €25) set their prices by demand; on a budget, admire their facades on Passeig de Gràcia for nothing.
Suggested accommodation: Sant Jordi Hostel Gràcia, Barcelona
Daily distance: about 10km on foot, plus metro.

Day 4: Montserrat (or Girona)
Take the R5 train from Plaça Espanya and then the Cremallera rack railway or the cable car up to the monastery, about €30 return from Barcelona (check the current fares). Walk the trails above the basilica for the views of the serrated rock. For a swap, take the fast train to Girona (from about 40 minutes) for its medieval walls and Jewish Quarter.
Suggested accommodation: Sant Jordi Hostel Gràcia, Barcelona
Daily distance: about 120km return by rail.

Day 5: Montjuïc and home
Walk up Montjuïc for the views over the port, then stroll down past the National Art Museum of Catalonia (MNAC). The Magic Fountain puts on free shows in the evening on a seasonal schedule, so check the current dates. If you have time, browse the Mercat de Sant Antoni before heading to the airport.
Daily distance: about 15km to the airport.

Metro tickets: The T-casual (10 trips, €13) can be shared between people, but it does not cover the airport. If you will use the metro all day, the Hola Barcelona travel card (from about €19 for 48 hours) includes the airport and unlimited travel.

Pickpockets: The metro, Las Ramblas and the beach are the hotspots. Keep your phone in a front pocket or zipped bag and never leave it on a café table.

Book early: With Gaudí's centenary in 2026, the Sagrada Família has been selling out up to two months ahead and hostels fill early. Avoid unofficial ticket resellers, who add a lot to the price.

5 DAYS: Madrid Without the Tourist Tax

Madrid is the cheapest of Spain's big cities to stay in and the easiest to love: three of the world's great art museums, a huge park, late nights that start at 11pm and a bocadillo de calamares for the price of a coffee. It also sits in the middle of the rail network, which makes Toledo and Segovia easy day trips. At about €52 a day, this is a five-day stay with one hostel and no lines to stand in if you book the free museum slots.

Read More

Day 1: Arrive in Madrid
Take the 24-hour Exprés Aeropuerto bus (about €5) or Cercanías into the city. Check in and eat at a local bar in La Latina; the Calle Cava Baja has the best cluster of tapas bars.
Suggested accommodation: Cat's Hostel, Madrid
Daily distance: about 15km from the airport.

Day 2: The old city
Start at the Plaza Mayor, with a bocadillo de calamares (squid sandwich) on the street behind it. Walk through the Royal Palace's free gardens (Sabatini and Campo del Moro), then end the day at the Templo de Debod, an Egyptian temple that has a free sunset view over the city. Hot chocolate and churros at San Ginés are a late-night Madrid ritual.
Suggested accommodation: Cat's Hostel, Madrid
Daily distance: about 8km on foot.

Day 3: The Golden Triangle of art
Pick two of the three museums. The Prado costs €15 and is free from 6pm to 8pm Monday to Saturday (5pm to 7pm on Sundays); book a free ticket online to skip the queue. The Reina Sofía, home of Picasso's Guernica, costs €12, is free from 7pm to 9pm Monday and Wednesday to Saturday, and is closed on Tuesdays. The Thyssen-Bornemisza (about €14) is free on Mondays from 12pm to 4pm for the permanent collection, with door tickets only. A Paseo del Arte card for all three costs about €33–35.
Suggested accommodation: Cat's Hostel, Madrid
Daily distance: about 6km on foot.

Day 4: Toledo
Take an Alsa coach from Plaza Elíptica (about an hour) or the Avant train from Atocha (about 35 minutes) to the walled hill city. Wander the old Jewish Quarter, see the cathedral and watch sunset from the Mirador del Valle across the river. For a swap, go to Segovia (about 30 minutes by Avant) for the Roman aqueduct and roast suckling pig.
Suggested accommodation: Cat's Hostel, Madrid
Daily distance: about 140km return.

Day 5: Retiro, Lavapiés and home
Spend the morning in Retiro Park, hire a rowing boat on the lake and see the Crystal Palace. If it's a Sunday or holiday, El Rastro flea market runs in La Latina until mid-afternoon. Then wander Lavapiés, the city's most multicultural neighbourhood, before heading to the airport.
Daily distance: about 15km to the airport.

Free museum hours: Free entry is the busiest time. Book the free tickets online where possible, and note that free slots, opening days and prices change, so confirm on each museum's site before you go.

Siesta and dinner times: Many kitchens close between about 4pm and 8pm and dinner rarely starts before 9pm. If you are hungry at 6pm, a bar with tapas or a bakery is your friend.

10 DAYS: The Big Four

The classic first trip to Spain: Barcelona, Madrid, Seville and Granada, linked by high-speed rail and one short coach, with a flight out of Málaga. It is the Spain most visitors want to see, done at a pace that gives each city enough time, for about €66 a day. Fly into Barcelona and out of Málaga.

Read More

Day 1: Arrive in Barcelona
Take the metro from the airport (the airport ticket is €5.90 one way). Walk through Gràcia and eat early.
Suggested accommodation: Sant Jordi Hostel Gràcia, Barcelona
Daily distance: about 15km from the airport.

Day 2: Barcelona
Walking tour of the Gothic Quarter and El Born, then Barceloneta beach, then the Bunkers del Carmel at sunset (free).
Suggested accommodation: Sant Jordi Hostel Gràcia, Barcelona
Daily distance: about 12km on foot.

Day 3: Gaudí
The Sagrada Família (€26 basic, €24 for under-30s and students; timed, online-only and sells out weeks ahead) and Park Güell's monumental zone (€18). The rest of Park Güell is free.
Suggested accommodation: Sant Jordi Hostel Gràcia, Barcelona
Daily distance: about 10km on foot, plus metro.

Day 4: Barcelona to Madrid
A high-speed train to the capital in 2.5 to 3 hours. Ouigo and Iryo are cheaper than Renfe's AVE on average; advance fares start around €19, but a typical advance fare is nearer €45–60. Evening tapas in La Latina.
Suggested accommodation: Cat's Hostel, Madrid
Daily distance: 620km by rail.

Day 5: Madrid
The Prado is free from 6pm to 8pm Monday to Saturday (book the free ticket online), and the Reina Sofía is free from 7pm to 9pm Monday and Wednesday to Saturday (closed Tuesdays). Spend the day in Retiro, the Plaza Mayor and the Templo de Debod at sunset.
Suggested accommodation: Cat's Hostel, Madrid
Daily distance: about 8km on foot.

Day 6: Madrid to Seville
A high-speed train south in about 2.5 hours, €35–60 if booked ahead. Walk through Santa Cruz at dusk.
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Hostel Sevilla, Seville
Daily distance: 530km by rail.

Day 7: Seville
The Real Alcázar (€15.50, book ahead; free for the last hour on Mondays with a reservation), the Plaza de España and the María Luisa Park, then flamenco at La Carbonería in the evening.
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Hostel Sevilla, Seville
Daily distance: about 10km on foot.

Day 8: Seville to Granada
A morning Alsa coach, about 3 hours and €18–25. Spend the evening on free tapas in the Realejo.
Suggested accommodation: Granada Inn Backpackers, Granada
Daily distance: 250km by coach.

Day 9: The Alhambra
Book the General ticket with Nasrid Palaces slot (about €22 online) weeks ahead. The Alcazaba reopened on 23 September 2026 on an adapted route after August's earthquakes, but visitor routes can change, and the cathedral and some churches were closed for checks, so confirm before you go.
Suggested accommodation: Granada Inn Backpackers, Granada
Daily distance: about 8km on foot.

Day 10: Granada to Málaga and home
A morning coach to Málaga, about 1.5 hours, then the Cercanías train to the airport.
Daily distance: 130km by coach, then 10km to the airport.

Accommodation (9 nights in dorms: Barcelona about €30, Madrid €27, Seville €23, Granada €20):
€230

Intercity transport (Barcelona–Madrid €45, Madrid–Seville €40, Seville–Granada €20, Granada–Málaga €12):
€117

Local transport and airport transfers:
€35

Food and drink (about €18/day):
€180

Activities (Sagrada Família €26, Park Güell €18, Alcázar €15.50, Alhambra €22, plus extras):
€100

Rail fares: On Madrid–Barcelona, average fares in late 2025 were about €61 on Ouigo, €77 on Iryo and €96 on Renfe's AVE. Renfe's cheap Avlo brand no longer runs on that route. Book early, and note that most Ouigo fares cannot be changed or refunded.

Flying instead: If you would rather skip a city to save time, you can fly between Barcelona and Seville or Málaga in about 1.5 hours. Check Ryanair and Vueling fares, and remember to add the cost of baggage and airport transfers.

2 WEEKS: The Shoestring Tour

The cheapest itinerary on the site, and a proper one: six cities in fourteen days, from Barcelona to Madrid via Valencia, Granada, Seville and Córdoba, with no flights inside Spain and no hire car. Everything moves by bus and train, you sleep in dorms with kitchens, breakfast comes from a supermarket and lunch is a menú del día. It works out at about €52 a day all in, and it still includes the Alhambra. The route is one-way, so fly into Barcelona and out of Madrid (or the reverse) — an open-jaw ticket usually costs little more than a return.

Read More

Day 1: Arrive in Barcelona
Take the metro from the airport (line L9 Sud, with the airport supplement) or the Aerobús if your hostel is near Plaça de Catalunya. Drop your bags, walk down to the sea at Barceloneta and eat early; Spanish dinners start late and the cheapest menus finish by 9pm.
Suggested accommodation: Sant Jordi Hostel Gràcia, Barcelona
Daily distance: about 15km from the airport.

Day 2: Barcelona on foot
Join a tip-based walking tour of the Gothic Quarter, then wander El Born, Santa Maria del Mar and the Ciutadella park. Finish at the Bunkers del Carmel, a free hilltop viewpoint with the best sunset in the city — bring supermarket beers and ignore the street sellers.
Suggested accommodation: Sant Jordi Hostel Gràcia, Barcelona
Daily distance: about 10km on foot.

Day 3: Gràcia, Montjuïc and the Gaudí exteriors
Spend the morning in Gràcia's squares, then see the Sagrada Família from outside (free) and the open surroundings of Park Güell. In the afternoon take the escalators up Montjuïc for the views and, in the evening, the free Magic Fountain show (check the current season's schedule). If you want to go inside the Sagrada Família it costs €26 for basic entry (€24 for under-30s and students) and is timed and online-only, but this itinerary skips it to protect the budget.
Suggested accommodation: Sant Jordi Hostel Gràcia, Barcelona
Daily distance: about 10km on foot, plus metro.

Day 4: Barcelona to Valencia
A morning train or bus down the coast, 3 to 4.5 hours depending on service. Train fares from around €25 if booked early; coach fares from around €15–25 on FlixBus or Alsa. Arrive in time for a walk through the Turia Gardens, the old river bed turned into a 9km park that runs through the city.
Suggested accommodation: Red Nest Hostel, Valencia
Daily distance: 350km by rail or coach.

Day 5: Valencia
Breakfast at the Mercado Central, then the street art of the Carmen quarter and the outside of the City of Arts and Sciences (free to look at). Take a city bus out to the Albufera lagoon for sunset and eat paella in its home territory; the proper version uses rabbit and chicken, not seafood.
Suggested accommodation: Red Nest Hostel, Valencia
Daily distance: about 25km including the Albufera.

Day 6: Valencia to Granada
The long day of the trip: Alsa runs a direct coach of roughly 7.5 to 8 hours, about €35–40 if booked ahead. Take food and a charged phone. Granada rewards you on arrival — in many of its bars a free tapa still arrives with every drink, so dinner can cost the price of two beers.
Suggested accommodation: Granada Inn Backpackers, Granada
Daily distance: about 500km by coach.

Day 7: The Alhambra
Book the General ticket with Nasrid Palaces slot on the official site as soon as your dates are fixed; it costs about €22 online and the palace slots sell out weeks ahead in season. Arrive for your slot, then explore the Generalife gardens and the Alcazaba, which reopened on 23 September 2026 after August's earthquakes on an adapted route. Watch the sun go down from the Mirador de San Nicolás in the Albaicín.
Suggested accommodation: Granada Inn Backpackers, Granada
Daily distance: about 8km on foot.

Day 8: Granada for free
Walk the Darro river path from Paseo de los Tristes, climb through the Sacromonte neighbourhood and eat free tapas in the Realejo. Several Granada monuments, including the cathedral, were closed for structural checks after the August earthquakes, so confirm opening before you plan to go in.
Suggested accommodation: Granada Inn Backpackers, Granada
Daily distance: about 8km on foot.

Day 9: Granada to Seville
A morning Alsa coach across Andalusia, about 3 hours and €18–25. Arrive in time for the Santa Cruz quarter at dusk, when the orange-scented alleys are at their best.
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Hostel Sevilla, Seville
Daily distance: 250km by coach.

Day 10: Seville
Plaza de España and the María Luisa Park in the morning, then cross the river to Triana for the market and a long lunch. In the evening, La Carbonería in Santa Cruz hosts informal flamenco in a free-entry bar; arrive early for a seat.
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Hostel Sevilla, Seville
Daily distance: about 10km on foot.

Day 11: Seville to Córdoba
A short hop on the train or coach, from 45 minutes to 2 hours and €10–25 depending on service. Spend the afternoon at the Mezquita-Catedral (€15, or €12 for students aged 15–26 and Youth Card holders, with a free early-morning slot — check the official site for current days and hours), the Roman Bridge and the white lanes of the Judería.
Suggested accommodation: Córdoba hostels
Daily distance: 140km by rail.

Day 12: Córdoba to Madrid
A morning high-speed train to the capital in about two hours. Book Ouigo or Iryo in advance for €15–35; the same ticket turned up on the day can cost three times as much. Settle in and walk down to the Plaza Mayor for a bocadillo de calamares, the city's cheap signature sandwich.
Suggested accommodation: Cat's Hostel, Madrid
Daily distance: 400km by rail.

Day 13: Madrid
Retiro Park in the morning, then the Prado (free 6–8pm Monday to Saturday) and the Reina Sofía (free 7–9pm Monday and Wednesday to Saturday, closed on Tuesdays) on their free evening slots; book the free tickets online to skip the queue. Watch sunset at the Templo de Debod and eat in Lavapiés. If it's a Sunday, spend the morning at El Rastro flea market.
Suggested accommodation: Cat's Hostel, Madrid
Daily distance: about 10km on foot.

Day 14: Madrid and home
Spend the morning in the Malasaña and La Latina neighbourhoods, then take the metro or Cercanías to the airport. Aim to be there at least two hours before an international flight.
Daily distance: about 15km to the airport.

Estimated Cost Breakdown (Per Person, 2026)

Based on dorm beds throughout and a solo traveller, with prices checked in October 2026. Flights to and from Spain are not included.

Accommodation (13 nights in dorms, about €25 a night on average, higher in Barcelona and Madrid, lower in Granada):
€325

Intercity transport (Barcelona–Valencia €20, Valencia–Granada €38, Granada–Seville €20, Seville–Córdoba €13, Córdoba–Madrid €25):
€116

Local transport and airport transfers:
€25

Food and drink (about €16/day: supermarket breakfast, menú del día lunch, free tapas in Granada):
€224

Activities (Alhambra €22, Mezquita-Catedral €15, one paid museum €8; everything else is free):
€45

Cheaper still: Hostel work exchange can cut the accommodation bill to almost nothing (typically a few hours’ work a day for a bed, with a minimum stay). Dropping Barcelona and starting in Madrid saves about €150. And in Granada, a drink with a free tapa is a legitimate dinner.

Rail fares: Spanish rail pricing follows demand. On Madrid–Barcelona, average fares in late 2025 were about €61 on Ouigo, €77 on Iryo and €96 on Renfe's AVE, with advance fares from around €19. Book as early as you can, and note that most Ouigo fares cannot be changed or refunded.

Pickpockets: Barcelona's metro, the Ramblas and the beach are the hotspots. Keep your phone in a front pocket or zipped bag, and never leave it on a café table.

2 WEEKS: The Flashpacker's Spain

The same country with the friction removed. You take high-speed trains rather than coaches, sleep in design hostels and boutique guesthouses in private rooms, eat dinner sitting down and book the experiences that are worth it: the Sagrada Família, a flamenco show, a cooking class, the Arab baths in Granada. It runs from Barcelona to Málaga via Madrid, Seville and Granada, and works out at roughly €114 a day. Fly into Barcelona and out of Málaga.

Read More

Day 1: Arrive in Barcelona
Take a taxi or the Aerobús to your hostel, drop your bags and walk through Gràcia, the neighbourhood where locals actually live. Book an early dinner and go to bed; tomorrow is a big day.
Suggested accommodation: Generator Barcelona (private room)
Daily distance: about 15km from the airport.

Day 2: Gaudí day
Book timed tickets online well ahead. The Sagrada Família costs €26 for basic entry including the audio guide (€24 for under-30s and students, €36 with a tower), and sells out weeks ahead in this centenary year. Park Güell's monumental zone now costs €18 (it rose sharply in 2026) and also needs a timed ticket; the rest of the park is free. Have a menú del día in Gràcia in between.
Suggested accommodation: Generator Barcelona (private room)
Daily distance: about 8km on foot, plus metro.

Day 3: Gothic Quarter and the Picasso Museum
Walk the Gothic Quarter in the morning, then the Picasso Museum in El Born (book ahead). In the evening take a tapas and vermouth tour, about €55, which is usually the best money you'll spend in the city.
Suggested accommodation: Generator Barcelona (private room)
Daily distance: about 10km on foot.

Day 4: Montserrat
A day trip to the serrated mountain monastery. The R5 train plus the Cremallera rack railway costs about €30 return from Barcelona, and the monastery and its walking trails reward the early start. Back in Barcelona for dinner.
Suggested accommodation: Generator Barcelona (private room)
Daily distance: about 120km return by rail.

Day 5: Barcelona to Madrid
A high-speed train to the capital in 2.5 to 3 hours. Choose a flexible fare (€55–80 on Iryo or AVE) if your plans might move; Ouigo is cheaper but non-changeable. Evening drinks and tapas in La Latina.
Suggested accommodation: The Hat Madrid (private room)
Daily distance: 620km by rail.

Day 6: Madrid's art triangle
The Prado (€15) and the Reina Sofía (€12, home of Picasso's Guernica) are close enough to do on the same day. Finish with a drink on the Círculo de Bellas Artes rooftop terrace (about €5 to go up) for the best free-ish skyline in the city.
Suggested accommodation: The Hat Madrid (private room)
Daily distance: about 9km on foot.

Day 7: Toledo day trip
Toledo's walled old town is 35 to 60 minutes from Madrid by Alsa coach or Avant train, around €12 return. Cathedral, synagogues, swords and marzipan. Back in Madrid for the evening.
Suggested accommodation: The Hat Madrid (private room)
Daily distance: about 140km return.

Day 8: Madrid to Seville
A high-speed train south in about 2.5 hours, €35–75 depending on how early you book. Check in, then walk through Santa Cruz at dusk.
Suggested accommodation: Samay Hostel Sevilla (private room)
Daily distance: 530km by rail.

Day 9: Seville's Alcázar and a flamenco night
Book the Real Alcázar ahead (€15.50) and arrive first thing, before the tour groups. Visit the cathedral (about €13) in the afternoon, and check whether the Giralda tower climb is open, as restoration work was announced for 2026. Finish with an evening flamenco show in a Triana tablao (about €30–40).
Suggested accommodation: Samay Hostel Sevilla (private room)
Daily distance: about 8km on foot.

Day 10: Cooking class and Triana
A half-day Andalusian cooking class (about €55–65) that starts with a market tour and ends with lunch. Spend the afternoon in Triana's ceramic workshops, then take it easy at the hostel's rooftop.
Suggested accommodation: Samay Hostel Sevilla (private room)

7 DAYS: Camino Francés, Sarria to Santiago

The last 115km of the Camino Francés is the most popular pilgrimage walk in the world, and the most sociable. You walk about 20–29km a day through Galician farmland, stop at village bars for coffee and tortilla, sleep in €10 hostels and eat the pilgrim menu with a table of strangers who, by the third night, are friends. It works out at about €40 a day. This page also covers cycling the Camino. For the whole 780km route see 3 Months: La Vuelta below, and for background see our Camino de Santiago page.

Read More

Day 1: Santiago to Sarria
Take a bus from Santiago to Sarria, usually via Lugo (about €15 in total; check the timetable, as a direct bus may run). Buy your pilgrim credential (the credencial) from the albergue or a church, and get your first stamp. You need two stamps a day on the last 100km to qualify for the Compostela certificate.
Suggested accommodation: The public Xunta albergue in Sarria (about €10), or a private albergue (about €15–30)
Daily distance: about 115km by bus.

Day 2: Sarria to Portomarín
The first stage, a gentle day through oak woods and stone hamlets, and the 100km marker at Ferreiros. You arrive in Portomarín over a bridge above the reservoir.
Suggested accommodation: The public Xunta albergue in Portomarín (about €10), or a private albergue
Daily distance: about 22km on foot.

Day 3: Portomarín to Palas de Rei
A longer climb in the morning, then rolling country. Many pilgrims stop for a snack at a village bar; do the same.
Suggested accommodation: The public Xunta albergue in Palas de Rei (about €10), or a private albergue
Daily distance: about 25km on foot.

Day 4: Palas de Rei to Arzúa
The longest day, with a famous detour: the octopus restaurants (pulperías) in Melide are the traditional lunch. It is the day with the most sore feet, so start early.
Suggested accommodation: The public Xunta albergue in Arzúa (about €10), or a private albergue
Daily distance: about 29km on foot.

Day 5: Arzúa to O Pedrouzo
A shorter day through eucalyptus forest. At O Pedrouzo, everyone is excited and nervous about tomorrow.
Suggested accommodation: The public Xunta albergue in O Pedrouzo (about €10), or a private albergue
Daily distance: about 19km on foot.

Day 6: O Pedrouzo to Santiago
Climb Monte do Gozo for the first view of the cathedral spires, then walk into the old town and the Praza do Obradoiro. Collect your Compostela certificate at the Pilgrim's Office (queues can be long), and attend the pilgrims' mass if you can.
Suggested accommodation: In Santiago, a budget albergue (for example the Seminario Menor, Fin del Camino or Santo Santiago; check current prices and opening)
Daily distance: about 20km on foot.

Day 7: Santiago, Finisterre or home
Spend the morning in the old town and the market. For a swap, take a bus to Finisterre, the headland the Romans thought was the end of the world, and watch the sunset there.
Daily distance: about 90km by bus to Finisterre.

Cycling the Camino

Pilgrims who cycle need to cover the last 200km (not 100km) to qualify for the Compostela, so the Sarria start does not count. Start in Ponferrada, about 200km from Santiago, and allow three or four days of 50–70km. Bikes are allowed on the same path, but some sections are steep or rocky and you may have to push, and walkers have priority. Hire a bike in Ponferrada or Santiago and ask about one-way returns, or bring your own.

I have not travelled this route in a wheelchair, so treat every detail as something to confirm with each venue before you go. We will expand this page with more routes and more kinds of access.

Take it easy: the riverside, Triana's market and an evening flamenco show at a venue that confirms access. Allow time for rest.
Suggested accommodation: Same hotel
Daily distance: about 4km.

Day 7: Seville to Málaga
A train to Málaga takes about 2 to 2.5 hours (check the timetable). Request assistance in advance again. Málaga's seafront promenade is flat and some city beaches offer adapted bathing services in summer; confirm with the city council.
Suggested accommodation: A hotel with a confirmed accessible room in Málaga
Daily distance: about 210km by rail.

Day 8: Málaga and home
A morning on the promenade and the port, then the Cercanías train or an accessible taxi to the airport.
Daily distance: about 10km to the airport.

Estimated Cost Breakdown (Per Person, 2026)

Based on two people sharing an accessible hotel room, with prices checked in October 2026. Flights to and from Spain are not included. Accessible rooms vary widely in price.

Accommodation (7 nights in an accessible double, about €45 per person per night):
€315

Transport (Madrid–Seville and Seville–Málaga by train, plus accessible taxis):
€140

Food and drink (about €25/day):
€200

Activities (museums and sights):
€60

Total estimated trip cost: ~€715 per person (approximately €89/day.)

Always confirm: Accessibility information changes, and a lift may be out of order on the day. Contact every hotel, venue and transport operator directly, and ask for details in writing.

Assistance dogs: Assistance dogs are legally allowed on public transport and in hotels in Spain, but check airline and ferry rules for your own dog before you travel.

Granada: Granada's Albaicín is steep and cobbled and is very difficult for wheelchair users, which is why this route leaves it out. If you want the Alhambra, check the official site for the accessible route first.

3 WEEKS: Spain Beyond the Guidebook

The Spain that most visitors never see: the Roman and medieval towns of Extremadura, the Spanish port of Cádiz, the two Spanish cities on the coast of North Africa (Ceuta and Melilla) and the desert of Almería, where the spaghetti westerns were filmed. It uses coaches, trains, two hire-car days and ferries, and works out at about €81 a day, because Ceuta and Melilla have few hostels. Fly into Madrid and out of Málaga. Read the safety note below before you go.

Read More

Day 1: Madrid to Cáceres
A train or coach to Extremadura, about 4 hours and about €25. Cáceres' walled old town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the best-preserved medieval centres in Europe.
Suggested accommodation: Budget pensiones and hostels in Cáceres (about €30)
Daily distance: about 300km by rail.

Day 2: Cáceres
Wander the old town at dawn, before the day trippers; it is empty and magnificent. Cáceres stood in for Braavos in Game of Thrones.
Suggested accommodation: Budget pensiones and hostels in Cáceres
Daily distance: about 5km on foot.

Day 3: Trujillo (hire a car)
Pick up a small hire car (you need one for the next two days, as public transport is thin). Trujillo's granite square and castle are half an hour away.
Suggested accommodation: Budget pensiones and hostels in Cáceres
Daily distance: about 100km return.

Day 4: Monfragüe to Mérida
Drive to Monfragüe National Park for griffon vultures and the Salto del Gitano cliff, then on to Mérida. Return the car in Mérida.
Suggested accommodation: Budget pensiones and hostels in Mérida (about €30)
Daily distance: about 200km.

Day 5: Mérida
Spain's best Roman ruins: the theatre and amphitheatre, the aqueduct and the bridge. A combined ticket covers the main sites (check current price).
Suggested accommodation: Budget pensiones and hostels in Mérida
Daily distance: about 5km on foot.

Day 6: Mérida to Seville
A coach south, about 2.5 hours and €15. You are in a big city for one night only, so eat well.
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Hostel Sevilla, Seville
Daily distance: about 200km by coach.

Day 7: Seville to Cádiz
A train to the white port city on its Atlantic peninsula, about 1 hour 40 minutes. Walk the seafront and eat fried fish.
Suggested accommodation: A hostel in Cádiz's old town
Daily distance: about 125km by rail.

Day 8: Cádiz to Algeciras
A coach along the coast, about 2.5 hours. Your base for tomorrow's ferry.
Suggested accommodation: A budget hostal in Algeciras
Daily distance: about 130km by coach.

Day 9: Algeciras to Ceuta
The ferry takes about 1 to 1.5 hours and costs from about €35 each way (fares on this route rose sharply in 2026). Ceuta is a Spanish city on the African coast, with a Spanish, Moroccan and Portuguese heritage. Start with the Royal Walls.
Suggested accommodation: A budget hotel or hostal in Ceuta (about €50–80); Ceuta has few hostels
Daily distance: about 20km by sea.

Day 10: Ceuta
Climb Monte Hacho for the views across the Strait to Gibraltar and the Rif mountains, and spend the afternoon on the city beaches.
Suggested accommodation: A budget hotel or hostal in Ceuta
Daily distance: about 10km.

Day 11: Ceuta to Málaga
Take the ferry back to Algeciras and a coach to Málaga (about 2 hours).
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Palace, Málaga
Daily distance: about 150km by sea and coach.

Day 12: Málaga to Almería
A coach east along the coast, about 3.5 to 4 hours. Almería's Alcazaba is the second-largest Moorish fortress in Spain (after the Alhambra).
Suggested accommodation: A hostel in Almería (about €25)
Daily distance: about 215km by coach.

Day 13: Cabo de Gata
A day in the Cabo de Gata natural park, one of the driest corners of Europe: empty beaches, volcanic cliffs and fishing villages. Public transport is limited, so take a seasonal bus or a tour.
Suggested accommodation: A hostel in Almería
Daily distance: about 35km each way.

Day 14: The Tabernas Desert
A bus to Tabernas, one of the driest places in Europe and often called its only desert, where the spaghetti westerns were filmed, about 45 minutes away. Explore the film-set theme parks and the badlands.
Suggested accommodation: A hostel in Almería
Daily distance: about 30km each way.

Day 15: Almería to Melilla
The ferry takes about 5.5 hours and costs from about €50 (check the sailing times, which vary by day). Melilla is Spain's other city on the African coast.
Suggested accommodation: A budget hotel or hostal in Melilla (about €50–70)
Daily distance: about 180km by sea.

Day 16: Melilla
Walk Melilla la Vieja, the old fortress city on the sea cliffs, then the Modernist buildings of the centre, often described as the finest outside Barcelona.
Suggested accommodation: A budget hotel or hostal in Melilla
Daily distance: about 8km on foot.

Day 17: Melilla
A day on the beaches and in the cafés. Keep this day free in case your ferry is delayed or cancelled by weather.
Suggested accommodation: A budget hotel or hostal in Melilla
Daily distance: about 5km on foot.

Day 18: Melilla to Málaga
The ferry back to the mainland, about 6 to 7 hours (check the schedule).
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Palace, Málaga
Daily distance: about 210km by sea.

Day 19: Málaga
Beach, the Picasso Museum and a last espeto supper.
Suggested accommodation: Oasis Backpackers' Palace, Málaga
Daily distance: about 8km on foot.

Day 20: Málaga and home
The Cercanías train to the airport.
Daily distance: about 10km to the airport.

Estimated Cost Breakdown (Per Person, 2026)

Based on dorms or budget rooms, with two days of car hire, and prices checked in October 2026. Flights to and from Spain are not included. Ceuta and Melilla have very few hostels, so budget hotels cost more.

Accommodation (19 nights: about €25–30 on the mainland and €60–65 in Ceuta and Melilla):
€693

Transport (coaches, trains and local transport €162, two days of car hire and fuel €105, ferries to Ceuta and Melilla €173):
€440

Food and drink (about €20/day):
€400

Activities (Mérida, Tabernas and extras):
€80

Total estimated trip cost: ~€1,610 per person (approximately €81/day.)

Border and safety: The Morocco land border has been unstable this year: in late July 2026, thousands of people crossed from Morocco into Ceuta and at least 18 died, according to news reports, and Morocco has since tightened security. This itinerary does not cross into Morocco. Check your government's travel advice before you go, and check the ferry schedules a few days before.

Ferries: There is no ferry between Ceuta and Melilla, so you go back through the mainland. Weather can cancel sailings, which is why Day 17 is a spare day.

Entry rules: Customs and tax rules differ in Ceuta and Melilla from the rest of Spain, and passport checks apply when you leave. Carry your passport at all times.
