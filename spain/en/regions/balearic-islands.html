Backpacking the Balearic Islands

[EES-lahs bah-leh-AH-rehs]

Say "the Balearics" and most people picture one thing: Ibiza at 5am. But this is really four different places. Ibiza is the party capital of the world. Mallorca is a big mix of city, mountains and beaches. Menorca is slow, green and quiet. Formentera is a tiny island of white sand where people go to recover. Between them they get more than 15 million visitors a year, and most of those people arrive for the sun, the sea and the nights.

If you're coming from the UK, the flights are short and cheap, and the weather is a big step up from home. Nights start late, and dinner is at 10pm. Clubs don't get busy until 2am, and the sun still comes up at the end of it. Now the bad news. This is the priciest part of Spain, and Ibiza is the priciest part of that. Backpacking here takes planning, because a drink in a big club can cost more than a night in a hostel.

The good news is that budget travel in the Balearics is very possible if you're smart. Go in May, June or September, when prices drop but the clubs and beaches are still open. Book hostels early, because there aren't many and they fill up fast. Cook for yourself, spend your days on free beaches and save your money for one or two big nights. Many backpackers also use Menorca and Formentera to cool down after a few days of Ibiza.

What Kind of Place Is This, Exactly?

The Balearic Islands are a group of islands in the western Mediterranean, off Spain's east coast. About 1.2 million people live here. The four main islands are Mallorca, the biggest, then Menorca, Ibiza and tiny Formentera. Palma, on Mallorca, is the capital. Ibiza and Formentera together are sometimes called the Pitiusas, or "pine islands". You can fly to Palma, Ibiza and Menorca, and ferries link all the islands to each other and to the mainland.

A Short History: Phoenicians, Pirates and Hippies

People built huge stone towers called talayots on Mallorca and Menorca more than 3,000 years ago. Carthaginian settlers founded Ibiza Town around 654 BC. The Romans arrived in 123 BC, and the Moors ruled for several centuries after that. King Jaume I of Aragon took Mallorca in 1229. Pirates raided the coasts for hundreds of years, which is why so many old watchtowers dot the cliffs. Hippies discovered Ibiza in the 1960s and stayed. In 1987 a group of British DJs came home from Ibiza with new ideas, and that helped spark the UK rave scene. The island has been the world's clubbing capital ever since.

The Language You'll Hear

Two official languages: Spanish and Catalan. Each island has its own version of Catalan, and you'll see it on road signs and menus. Almost everyone in the resorts speaks English, especially in Ibiza and Magaluf, so you can get by easily. Learn a few Spanish words anyway, and add two Catalan ones: "bon dia" for good morning, and "gràcies" for thank you. Locals notice, and they like it.

Ibiza or Eivissa? Mallorca or Majorca?

They're the same places with different spellings. Eivissa is the Catalan name for Ibiza, and you'll see it on road signs. Mallorca is the Spanish and local spelling, while Majorca is the old English one. The same goes for Menorca, which English speakers sometimes write as Minorca, and Mahón, which is Maó in Catalan. This guide uses the Spanish spellings, but if you searched for Majorca or Minorca, you're in the right place.

Understanding the Balearics: The Islands & Zones

Ibiza Town (Eivissa)

The island's main town has a walled old quarter called Dalt Vila, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Steep streets lead up to the cathedral and the views. Down by the harbour, bars and restaurants stay busy until late, and the stylish crowd poses for the cameras. The Pacha club is in the town itself.

Sant Antoni (San Antonio)

The classic party town for young British travellers. It has the "West End" strip of loud bars and street promoters, plus the famous sunset strip along the bay, where crowds watch the sun go down with a drink. It's cheaper and rowdier than Ibiza Town, and it has the most budget beds on the island.

Playa d'en Bossa & The Big Clubs

A long beach next to Ibiza Town, lined with huge hotels and beach clubs. This is where the big daytime parties happen. Many of the island's most famous nightclubs are close by, including Hï Ibiza and DC10, and Amnesia is a short drive away. It's loud, packed and expensive.

Ibiza's Quiet North & East

Away from the clubs, Ibiza is a different island. Pine forests, small coves and sleepy villages fill the north and east. Santa Eulària and Es Canar are calm family spots with hippy markets. Benirràs beach is famous for its Sunday sunset drummers. Hire a scooter or take a bus to find them.

Mallorca

The biggest island has something for everyone. Palma is a lovely city with a huge cathedral, old streets and a lively food and bar scene. Magaluf and Playa de Palma are the party resorts, with cheap drinks and a big British crowd. The Serra de Tramuntana mountains are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and they're great for hiking and cycling. The east coast has pretty coves with clear turquoise water.

Menorca

The quiet, green island. It's a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve with fewer high-rise hotels, calm beaches and lots of countryside. The old towns of Maó and Ciutadella have harbours full of small bars. Britain ran Menorca for much of the 18th century, and you can still see it in the Georgian buildings and the local gin.

Formentera

Ibiza's tiny neighbour is only a short ferry ride away. It has some of the whitest sand and clearest water in Europe. It's flat and easy to explore by bike, with beach bars and not much else. Many people come for a day trip from Ibiza, and some stay a few nights to recharge.

A Note on Ibiza's Party Scene

Ibiza's clubbing season runs from about May to early October, with opening parties at the start and closing parties at the end. The biggest names play at Pacha, Amnesia, DC10, Hï Ibiza and Ushuaïa, and line-ups change every year. Big clubs are expensive and take over your whole night, so plan ahead, buy tickets early and don't expect to fit more than a few big nights into a week.

Balearic Islands FAQs for Backpackers

When is the best time to go?

May, June and September are best. The weather is warm, the clubs are open and prices are lower. July and August are the hottest, busiest and most expensive months, and beds can run out. Clubs close from early October until May, and many beach bars and small hostels shut with them. Winter is quiet, mild and good for hiking, but not for partying.

Which island should I pick?

For the parties, pick Ibiza. For a mix of city, mountains and beaches, pick Mallorca. For quiet, empty coves, pick Menorca. For white sand and a slow pace, pick Formentera. Many backpackers fly into Palma or Ibiza, then hop to a second island by ferry or a cheap flight.

Can I really do Ibiza on a budget?

Yes, but you need a plan. Book your hostel months ahead. Buy drinks and snacks at a supermarket instead of a beach bar. Buy club tickets online in advance, because they cost more on the door. Go to a daytime party instead of a night one, eat a cheap set lunch, and take the bus instead of a taxi. Finally, spend sunsets on free beaches.

What does it cost to go out?

In an ordinary bar, a small beer costs about €3 to €5. Inside a big club, a single drink can cost €15 or more. Tickets for the biggest Ibiza clubs often start at €50 to €100, and they rise for star DJs. Magaluf and Playa de Palma are cheaper, and Palma's local bars are cheaper again.

How do I get around?

Each island has a bus network that covers the main towns and beaches, and some run a night bus to the big clubs in summer. Taxis are hard to find at closing time and cost a lot. Ferries link the islands, and Formentera is about 30 to 40 minutes by fast ferry from Ibiza Town. For hidden coves, share a hire car with people from your hostel. Think hard before you hire a scooter, and read our safety section first.

Do I need to speak Spanish?

Not much. English is common in the resorts and the clubs, and most hostel staff speak it well. Away from the tourist areas, Spanish or Catalan helps. A few words go a long way.

Is the tap water safe?

It's generally safe, but it can taste salty, especially on Ibiza and Formentera, where fresh water is scarce. Most people drink bottled water. Please don't waste water, as the islands often struggle in summer.

Is it safe?

Mostly, yes. Violent crime against tourists is rare. The real dangers come from partying: drugs, heavy drinking, hot weather, balconies and scooters. See our full safety section below, and please read the part about drugs.

Staying Safe & Travelling Well in the Balearic Islands

The Balearics are safe for most backpackers. Violent crime is rare, and the police are used to young tourists. Most of the risks come from the party scene itself, and from the heat. A little common sense keeps your trip fun.

Drugs: The Straight Talk

Ibiza's club scene means drugs will be offered to you. Dealers approach people on the street, outside clubs, on beaches and around Sant Antoni's West End. Please don't buy from them. Pills and powders sold as MDMA, cocaine or ketamine are often something else, or far stronger than you expect. Every summer, young tourists end up in hospital, and some die. Mixing drugs with alcohol, heat and no sleep makes it much worse. So-called "pink cocaine" is not a single drug at all, and nobody can tell you what is in it.

The law is serious too. In Spain, using or carrying drugs in public can bring an on-the-spot fine, starting at €601 and rising to €30,000. Police search people near clubs and beaches. Selling drugs, even to friends, can mean prison. Spain is not as relaxed about this as some visitors think. If you choose to take drugs, you take the risks yourself, and your travel insurance may not pay out.

Look after your friends. If someone is very unwell, call 112 at once, stay with them and tell the medics what they took. They are there to help, not to judge. Never leave your drink alone, and watch for spiking, which can happen in any crowded bar or club.

Alcohol and Party-Zone Rules

Ibiza and Mallorca have rules for some party areas, including Sant Antoni, Playa d'en Bossa, Magaluf and Playa de Palma. These can limit late-night alcohol sales in shops, open bars, organised pub crawls and party boats. Drinking in the street can bring a fine. The rules change, so ask at your hostel. Spain's drink-drive limit is also lower than in England and Wales, so don't drink and drive.

Balconing: Please Don't

Balconing means jumping from, or climbing between, hotel and apartment balconies, often towards a pool. It has killed and badly injured many tourists, mostly young men, in Magaluf, Sant Antoni and Playa de Palma. It looks like a dare, but it's a very quick way to end your holiday. Your insurance may refuse to pay, and you can be thrown out of your accommodation.

Heat, Sun and Water

Summer is hot, and the sun is stronger than you think, especially after a late night. Use high-factor sunscreen, drink lots of water and take breaks in the shade. Cliff jumping is popular but dangerous, so never jump after drinking, and check how deep the water is first. Check the beach flag as well: green means fine, yellow means take care, and red means stay out.

Scooters and Quad Bikes

Accidents are common among tourists, and the roads are busy and unfamiliar. You need the right licence, and a helmet is the law. Insurance may not pay if you've been drinking or aren't properly licensed. Many hire shops take a big deposit and charge for scratches, so take photos of the bike before you leave.

Tickets, Taxis and Petty Theft

Fake club tickets are sold online and on the street. Buy from the club's own website or from an official partner. Use licensed taxis, and agree on the route before you get in. Phones and wallets get stolen from unattended beach towels and crowded dance floors, so use lockers and keep your valuables close.

Looking After the Islands

Local people have protested against overtourism in recent summers, and housing and water are real problems. Be a good guest. Keep noise down at night and take your rubbish home. Don't walk around town in swimwear, as some places fine you for it. Don't sleep on beaches or camp in the wild, because it's illegal and can bring a fine. Never anchor or swim over the seagrass meadows, which protect the water and are protected by law. A small tourist tax is charged on most accommodation, including hostels, so keep a few euros ready.

Quick Facts

Best Time To Visit:
May, June and September for good weather, open clubs and lower prices. July and August are hot, busy and expensive. Clubs run from about May to early October.
Best For:
Superclubs and beach parties, hidden coves, hiking and cycling, and island-hopping.
Most Popular Experience:
Watching the sunset in Sant Antoni, then dancing until dawn in Ibiza.
Most Under-rated Experience:
Menorca's Camí de Cavalls: a 185 km coastal path of quiet coves and clear water, free to walk.
Best Free Experiences:
Sunset at Benirràs beach, wandering the walled old town of Ibiza, the hippy markets, and the free beaches and hidden coves of Mallorca and Menorca.
Ideal Stay:
7 to 14 days: a week on Ibiza for the party, or split your time with Mallorca. Add Menorca or Formentera for quiet days.
Budget Level:
The most expensive region in Spain for backpackers. Expect roughly €60–€130 per day in summer, and less in spring and autumn. Ibiza costs the most.
Getting Around:
Fly into Palma, Ibiza or Menorca, or take a ferry from the mainland. Buses cover the main towns and beaches, and a shared hire car helps for hidden coves.
Best Photo Spots:
Es Vedrà rock from Cala d'Hort, Palma's cathedral from the seafront, Ibiza Town's old walls at sunset, and the turquoise water at Cala Macarella in Menorca.

1. Ibiza: The Party Capital
(Non-Negotiable)

Even if clubbing isn't your thing, Ibiza is worth seeing once. It's the world's party capital for a reason, and the whole island is built around it.

The Superclubs

Pacha, Amnesia, DC10, Hï Ibiza and Ushuaïa are the big names. Each has its own style, from huge light shows to sweaty terraces and daytime garden parties. Doors open around midnight, the biggest nights run from 2am to 6am, and tickets often start at €50 to €100. Check the line-ups before you book, as they change every year.

Beach Clubs & Sunsets

By day, the party moves to the beach. Playa d'en Bossa has big beach clubs with DJs and pool parties. In Sant Antoni, the bars along the sunset strip are packed every evening, as everyone watches the sun drop into the sea. Prices are high, so go for the view and one drink.

Read More

Dalt Vila, Ibiza Town

The walled old town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and it's a quiet change from the clubs. Climb the steep cobbled streets to the cathedral and the ramparts for the best view of the harbour. Walking around is free, and sunset from the walls is hard to beat.

Opening and Closing Parties

The season starts with opening parties in May and ends with closing parties in late September or early October. They're packed with regulars and DJs, and often cheaper than peak summer. They're also a good time to find a bed.

2. Adrenaline

Deep-water soloing in Mallorca: Climb sea cliffs with deep water below. If you fall, you just splash into the sea. Guided trips are available for beginners.

Cycling the Serra de Tramuntana: Mallorca is a world-famous cycling island, and the mountain roads have stunning views. Hire a road bike for a day, or try the switchback road down to Sa Calobra.

Diving and snorkelling: The water is clear and the seabed is rich in fish. Ibiza, Formentera and Mallorca all have dive schools, and boat trips run to the Cabrera national park.

Kayaking and coasteering: Paddle to hidden coves that you can't reach on foot, or jump from the rocks on a guided coasteering trip. A guide will pick safe spots and keep you out of trouble.

3. The Hidden Coves & Quiet Islands (The Days That Change Everything)

Most people see the Balearics through a haze of beach clubs. The quiet side is why many backpackers fall in love with them.

Mallorca's Coves

The east and south of Mallorca have small sandy coves, known as calas, with clear turquoise water. Caló des Moro is one of the prettiest. Es Trenc is a long, wild beach with soft white sand. Go early, as the best spots fill up by mid-morning in summer.

Menorca's Camí de Cavalls

An old path runs all the way around Menorca's coast, about 185 km in total. Walk it in short sections from cove to cove, with a swim at every stop. It's free, quiet and one of the best things to do in Spain on a budget.

Formentera

Take a fast ferry from Ibiza Town and hire a bike. The island is flat, so riding is easy. Ses Illetes is the most famous beach, with white sand and calm, clear water. It's pretty busy in summer, but it's still worth a day.

A note on Ibiza's quieter side: Ibiza has beautiful coves too, including Cala Comte for sunsets and Cala d'Hort, which looks out at Es Vedrà, a huge rock island wrapped in myths. Come early in the day, before the beach clubs fill up.

4. Free Balearics (Zero Euros)

Beaches and coves: Almost every beach on the islands is free, and the wild coves are the best. Bring water, snacks and shade, because many have no shops.

Sunset at Benirràs, Ibiza: On summer Sundays, drummers gather on this pretty beach to play at sunset. People dance, swim and watch the sun go down. It costs nothing, and it's a wonderful change from the clubs.

Sunset in Sant Antoni: You don't need to buy a drink to watch. Find a spot on the rocks along the bay and enjoy the show.

Dalt Vila and the hippy markets: Wandering the old town of Ibiza is free. So is browsing the hippy markets, at Las Dalias on Saturdays and at Es Canar on Wednesdays in summer.

Hiking in Mallorca and Menorca: The Serra de Tramuntana has free trails with huge views. Some walkers use the GR 221, a long mountain route with cheap refuges. In Menorca, the Camí de Cavalls costs nothing.

Palma's old town: Walk the lanes, look up at the cathedral and stroll along the seafront. It's free, and it's a great place to spend an afternoon.

5. Nightlife: How It Actually Works

The Balearics keep very late hours, and Ibiza is the latest of all. Dinner starts at 10pm, bars fill up around midnight, and big clubs don't get going until 2am. Plan a nap in the afternoon and eat a proper meal before you go.

For Ibiza's big nights

Pick one or two clubs that match your music and buy tickets online. Daytime parties at places like Ushuaïa and DC10 can be cheaper than late-night events, and you still get world-class DJs. Plan your way home too, because taxis are scarce at closing time. A summer night bus runs to some of the big clubs, so check the times.

For a cheaper night

Sant Antoni's bars have drink deals, and the West End is the cheapest way to party in Ibiza. Buy drinks at a supermarket and have a few at your hostel first, then head out. Keep it inside, since drinking in the street can bring a fine.

For Mallorca's party spots

Magaluf is loud, cheap and very British, with a long strip of bars and clubs. Playa de Palma is similar, with a bit more German flavour. For something more local, try the bars around Palma's old town and the Santa Catalina area.

For a calmer night

Menorca's harbours in Maó and Ciutadella have plenty of friendly bars. On Formentera, watch the sunset from a beach bar and then call it a night.

6. The Big Hits (Things You Must Not Skip)

Dalt Vila, Ibiza Town

Covered in full above, and it's the best non-clubbing thing to do in Ibiza. Go at sunset.

Palma Cathedral (La Seu)

A giant Gothic cathedral right on the seafront, with one of the largest rose windows in the world. It looks even better from the water at night. Entry costs a few euros.

The Serra de Tramuntana

A mountain range along Mallorca's north-west coast, with stone villages, olive terraces and sea views. Drive, cycle or hike the trails. The old wooden train from Palma to Sóller is a fun way to see it.

Day trip: Formentera

A quick ferry from Ibiza Town gets you to white sand and clear water. Hire a bike, ride to Ses Illetes and swim. It's the best way to recover after a big night.

7. Live Sport & Culture

RCD Mallorca football: Mallorca's football club has been a La Liga regular in recent seasons. A match at Son Moix stadium is good fun and much cheaper than a big mainland game.

Sant Joan, Ciutadella (Menorca): In late June, riders on black horses prance through the old town, and crowds squeeze into the narrow streets. It's loud, wild and totally free to watch.

Moors and Christians, Mallorca: Towns like Sóller and Pollença stage mock battles with costumes, noise and fireworks. Sóller's is in May, and Pollença's is in early August.

Ibiza's opening and closing parties: Not sport, but the best live culture on the islands. They mark the start and end of the season, and the island feels different when the crowds are smaller and the locals come out to play.
